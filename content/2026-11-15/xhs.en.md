**11.15｜Tech, Markets and Global News**

Beyond AI applications, today’s stories span asset prices, international talks and new drug approvals. These seven developments bring the key points into focus👇

1. **🖥️ Computing capacity keeps expanding as valuations face scrutiny**

   According to the Financial Times, stocks tied to data center construction have shed a combined $4.2 trillion in market value. Meanwhile, Samsung is planning to build data centers, and the U.S. Congress is pushing for stricter AI chip export rules.

   Three things to watch together: construction plans, market valuations and policy constraints.

2. **🤖 AI assistants: Can they get the job done?**

   Meta’s Muse now offers phone calls on behalf of its highest-tier subscribers, including contacting U.S. businesses and insurers and making reservations. Subscriptions start at $19.99 a month. But some businesses simply hang up on AI callers.

   Nvidia’s SkillEvaluator tackles the developer side, using quality thresholds, semantic overlap detection and task evaluations to check whether agent skills actually work.

   One serves users, the other developers. Both ultimately face the same question: How much do they improve task completion rates?

3. **📊 Bonds and Bitcoin have different price drivers**

   Emerging-market bond issuance has fallen 41%, with scarce new supply pushing bond prices higher. Bitcoin, meanwhile, has dropped to a four-month low as forced liquidations accelerate.

   The keys to understanding these moves are “shrinking supply” and “liquidation of leveraged positions,” respectively.

4. **🏘️ Hong Kong property: Watch financing and dilution**

   New World Development has proposed a $3.2 billion rights issue, seeking to raise funds by offering shares to existing shareholders.

   Watch how much the funding strengthens its finances, and how shareholders’ decisions to participate affect their ownership stakes.

5. **🌍 Global affairs: Two developments to keep tracking**

   Zelenskyy said the U.S. had explored a Ukrainian troop withdrawal from Donbas in exchange for Ukraine joining NATO. **The U.S. has not confirmed this**. Key questions are whether Washington formally endorses the proposal, whether NATO can reach a consensus, and how the security guarantees would be put into practice.

   In the Red Sea region, the Financial Times reports that Saudi-backed Yemeni forces have retreated, giving the Houthis an advantage in the local balance of power. Changes along the coast could affect market assessments of shipping risks.

6. **💊 New medicines: A setback for U.S. approval**

   According to Bloomberg, the U.S. FDA has rejected Summit’s application for a lung cancer drug developed in China.

   This sets back its U.S. commercialization plans. Watch for further regulatory details and the company’s response.

7. **🇬🇧 UK inflation shapes interest-rate expectations**

   Bloomberg’s analysis suggests that UK price data could strengthen the case for another central bank rate hike.

   The key word is “could”: What matters next is how inflation actually develops and how the central bank assesses it.
