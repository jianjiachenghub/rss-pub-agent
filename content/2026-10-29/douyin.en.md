Does stronger AI mean bigger profits? Today’s key question is whether AI spending can deliver real returns—a test that businesses and markets now share.

GitHub’s new Copilot lets multiple agents work in parallel. But the actual productivity gains depend on whether their work can be merged, whether reviews can keep up, and how many credits each task costs.

That trade-off is showing up in earnings, too: Unitree’s third-quarter revenue rose nearly 70%, but net profit fell about 17%. The company says the main reason was increased spending on computing power and large AI model research and development.

Investors are asking the same question about returns. Reports say Alphabet’s earnings missed expectations, sending its shares to a record drop. Meanwhile, the company is increasing AI capital spending, putting even more focus on how that investment will turn into revenue.

So when you use AI, look at the cost of completing a task. When you assess an AI company, look at its ability to turn investment into profit. In the next phase, those who deliver real returns will make the strongest case.
