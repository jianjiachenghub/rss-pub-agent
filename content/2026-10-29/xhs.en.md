**10.29 Daily Brief｜Tech Spends, Markets Keep Score**

Beyond developer tool updates, today’s stories to watch include robotics companies’ profitability, U.S. and European interest rates, and Europe’s crude oil supply. Here are 5 highlights👇

🧑‍💻 **Developer Tools: Multiple Agents, Bigger Budget Questions**

GitHub has launched a new Copilot app supporting multi-model orchestration, parallel agent sessions, and seamless handoffs across devices; Doubao Work has also opened third-party model configuration to enterprise users.

Teams now have more choices, but efficiency still depends on whether completed tasks merge smoothly, code reviews keep pace, and how much usage allowance each task consumes.

Heavy users, take note: Starting October 30, Codex and Work allowances on the $200 Pro plan will reportedly drop from 20 times those of Plus to 10 times, with no change to the monthly price.

🤖 **Robotics: Revenue Rises, Profits Fall**

Unitree reported third-quarter revenue of RMB 665 million, up 69.93% year over year; net profit attributable to shareholders was RMB 113 million, down 17.37%. The company attributed the decline mainly to increased spending on computing resources and large-model R&D.

The next question is whether that spending will translate into more deliveries and revenue. Rapid revenue growth alone does not yet mean profitability is improving.

📉 **Tech Markets: Investors Want Revenue to Materialize**

Bloomberg reported that Alphabet’s earnings missed expectations, sending its shares to a record drop; the Financial Times also reported that the company will increase AI capital spending. ServiceNow shares fell sharply as well after its sales outlook came in below expectations.

Taken together, these stories point to a question markets are pressing: As spending keeps rising, can future revenue keep pace? Model capabilities and product buzz ultimately have to stand up to financial scrutiny.

🏦 **U.S. and European Economies: Resilient Growth Limits Hopes for Easing**

The U.S. economy grew at its fastest pace in a year in the third quarter, supported by consumer spending and business investment. Financial Times analysis suggests that strong growth and inflation will keep the Federal Reserve’s monetary policy tight.

The European Central Bank held rates steady at this meeting; separately, Bloomberg cited people familiar with the matter as saying officials are prepared to raise rates in December if the inflation outlook fails to improve.

The distinction matters: **The current rate decision has been announced; a December hike remains conditional.**

🛢️ **Energy: Europe Plans to Release 42 Million Barrels of Crude**

France says Europe plans to release 42 million barrels of crude oil. However, the available summary gives no specific timeline, participating countries, or implementation details.

For now, watch the pace of the release: How much oil reaches the market, and how quickly, will determine the size of the supply cushion. The announcement alone does not mean oil prices will necessarily fall.
