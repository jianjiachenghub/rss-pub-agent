Today’s most important signal: AI competition is shifting from “who has the strongest model” to “who controls the talent, the access points, and the execution systems.”

Nvidia is reportedly willing to pay $6 billion to license Poolside’s models while bringing on more than 100 of its employees. It wouldn’t even need to acquire the entire company to absorb its core technology and team into its ecosystem. “Licensing plus hiring” could become a new playbook for AI acquisitions.

Apple is also refocusing on access points: cutting parts of its VR team and shifting resources toward Siri AI and lightweight smart glasses. This suggests bulky headsets are giving way to more everyday AI wearables.

Development is changing too. DeepSeek has launched a multimodal API with stronger visual understanding and tool use, while OpenAI has open-sourced the underlying Codex Harness, allowing companies to embed agent loops directly into their own products.

So when assessing AI companies going forward, don’t just watch the model leaderboards. The real differentiators will be top talent, access to end users, and whether agents can complete real-world tasks safely and reliably.
