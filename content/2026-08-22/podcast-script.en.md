For startups, talent and models are also beginning to be valued separately. When assessing similar deals in the future, we cannot look only at how much the company sold for. Bulky headsets are becoming less important, while glasses that can be worn all day, work alongside a smartphone, and integrate an AI assistant are moving higher up the list. One lowers the barrier to multimodal integration, while the other gives developers more control over agent loops. The next stage of competition may no longer be just about model benchmark scores, but about who can complete real-world tasks reliably, at lower cost and with less latency.

Host A: Once businesses actually deploy agents, permission issues quickly follow. AWS’s proposed Bedrock AgentCore Gateway seeks to manage how agents access enterprise tools through four stages: connecting, controlling, cataloging, and hardening.

Host B: That may sound like infrastructure, but it is crucial. Once an agent can query databases, send emails, or modify business systems, companies care about more than whether it can do the job. They also need to know what it is allowed to do, who approved it, and whether problems can be traced afterward. Permissions and auditing are becoming essential layers for real-world agent deployment.

Host B: That is why the upcoming Jackson Hole meeting is drawing so much attention. Markets do not just want to hear whether interest rates will be cut. They also want to know how the Federal Reserve describes the neutral rate, its tolerance for inflation, and the pace of balance-sheet reduction. If the language is hawkish, the dollar and long-term Treasury yields may continue to find support, making it difficult for financing pressure on businesses and households to ease quickly.
