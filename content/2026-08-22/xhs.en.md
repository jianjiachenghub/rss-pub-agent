# Today’s Must-Read Stories Go Beyond AI

August 22 was packed with developments. The key themes to watch are shifts in how we access technology, the global cost of capital, and the simultaneous rise in trade and geopolitical risks.

🤖 AI & Technology

Nvidia is reportedly set to pay $6 billion to license Poolside’s AI models and offer jobs to more than 100 of its employees.

This may represent a new form of consolidation in the AI industry: instead of acquiring an entire company, a buyer can secure its model licenses and core talent directly. Going forward, an AI startup’s model assets and team may matter more than its revenue when assessing its value.

Apple is also placing new bets: it is cutting part of its VR development team and shifting resources toward Siri AI and lightweight smart glasses. The next computing gateway may not be a bulky headset, but AI glasses that can be worn all day and work seamlessly with a smartphone.

DeepSeek has launched a multimodal API, while OpenAI has open-sourced Codex Harness. One makes it easier for models to “see images and carry out tasks”; the other lets developers embed agent loops directly into products. AI competition is shifting from “who can chat better” to “who can complete work more reliably.”

Businesses are also catching up on security. AWS has introduced a governance solution for agent access to tools, showing that when enterprises deploy agents, permissions, auditing, and security boundaries are now just as important as model capabilities.

💰 Markets & Macro

U.S. stocks rose and Bitcoin approached $80,000, but U.S. Treasury yields remain near multi-year highs.

This means risk appetite has returned, while the cost of capital has not fallen significantly. The upcoming Jackson Hole meeting will be crucial: markets are watching not only whether rates will be cut, but also how the Federal Reserve views long-term interest rates, its tolerance for inflation, and the pace of balance-sheet reduction.

Around $40 trillion in U.S. debt, mortgage rates of 6.7%, and diesel prices of $5 per gallon are also putting simultaneous pressure on government finances, housing, and transportation. High debt combined with high interest rates is becoming a real constraint on growth.

The Treasury’s bond operations have also revived the “debasement trade,” and gold, the dollar, and long-term bonds may continue to fluctuate in response to debt supply.

🌍 Trade & Geopolitics

The United States has imposed 50% tariffs on certain Canadian goods, and Canada says it will retaliate.

The impact may first spread to the automotive, metals, and cross-border manufacturing industries. Businesses now need to assess not whether tariffs will happen, but how long the dispute will last and which goods may receive exemptions.

Meanwhile, Iran’s president has signaled a willingness to end the war with the United States, while stressing that negotiations must begin from a “position of strength.” There are signs of a possible ceasefire, but bargaining leverage and negotiating terms will still determine where the situation goes.

🪙 Crypto Regulation

Even if the U.S. CLARITY Act fails to pass, regulators may still use their existing authority to establish rules. Binance’s latest round of scrutiny in the UAE also shows that a so-called “crypto-friendly jurisdiction” does not mean light regulation.

The crypto industry’s main focus should not be any single bill, but how the boundaries for trading platforms, token issuance, and custody services will gradually be defined through administrative rules and enforcement cases.

In one sentence:

The biggest shift today is that AI is beginning to compete for talent, access points, and execution capabilities; markets are being pulled between renewed risk appetite and high interest rates; and trade, regulatory, and geopolitical risks have not gone away.
