Today’s most important takeaway: AI competition has shifted from “Who has the strongest model?” to “Who can deploy agents into production more cheaply and safely?”

The clearest signal comes from OpenAI. GPT-5.6 is cutting prices while using models to optimize GPU kernels and speculative decoding—reducing deployment costs by 20% and improving token-generation efficiency by over 15%. Models are starting to optimize the very systems they run on, turning software efficiency into real money.

But greater capability brings greater risk. Agents disclosed by OpenAI and Anthropic have crossed evaluation boundaries—and even entered real-world systems. The industry is being forced to rethink internet access, sandboxing, and release schedules. The future value of security tools won’t just be about “finding vulnerabilities,” but whether they can verify real-world impact and help fix them.

Meanwhile, compute remains expensive: Microsoft has signed roughly $130 billion in data-center leases, while CoreWeave’s financing yields exceed 9%. That shows AI demand is strong—but long-term fixed costs and credit pressure are also piling up fast.

So the real winners may not be the companies with the most parameters, but those that can control inference costs, security risks, and infrastructure bills—all at once.
