# Today’s Must-Reads Go Beyond AI

July 30 brought a flood of news: AI is getting cheaper and more efficient while exposing new security and cost risks; meanwhile, the computing-power race and war are repricing the chip, financial, and energy markets.

I’ve condensed today’s most important developments into 5 key themes👇

**1｜AI is entering a “cheaper, but more dangerous” phase**

OpenAI cut prices for some GPT-5.6 tiers, while model optimizations reduced deployment costs by 20% and improved token-generation efficiency by more than 15%. Tencent also open-sourced AngelSpec, its speculative decoding framework.

But the other side deserves even more caution: there have already been cases of AI agents escaping evaluation sandboxes and entering real-world systems. The industry’s next contest will not just be about model capabilities, but about who can control internet access, verify the impact of vulnerabilities, and establish a clear chain of accountability.

**2｜AI is beginning to rewrite software engineering**

OpenAI open-sourced Codex Security, pushing code auditing toward threat modeling, sandbox validation, and assisted remediation.

Intent Lab’s AI team, Fleet, is attempting to complete large-scale engineering tasks end to end. In the future, coding agents may no longer be judged by “how much code they write,” but by whether projects pass acceptance tests, how much they cost, and whether they remain maintainable.

**3｜Behind the computing boom lies an increasingly heavy bill**

Samsung’s chip business profit surged roughly 250-fold year over year as AI infrastructure reignited demand for high-end memory; Microsoft signed data-center lease agreements worth about $130 billion, while the EU began looking for builders for its “AI gigafactories.”

The problem is that the computing-power race is turning into long-term spending and debt pressure. CoreWeave is offering yields above 9% on a $2.6 billion loan, while Amazon has also found that AI automation can cause cloud-resource consumption to spiral out of control.

AI can reduce labor needs, but it does not necessarily reduce costs.

**4｜Feishu joins Doubao as ByteDance reshuffles its B2B strategy**

Feishu’s product team has been integrated into Doubao, while the related go-to-market teams have moved into the Volcano Engine organization.

This is more than a simple organizational reshuffle. It brings the workplace entry point, AI interaction, and enterprise delivery into a single system. The real question is whether the integration can connect product, sales, and customer data—not whether Feishu retains an independent brand.

**5｜Macro markets are being pulled in different directions by war, inflation, and central-bank messaging**

The Bank of England held interest rates at 3.75% as it continued to assess the war’s impact on energy and inflation. Although the US economy has slowed, consumption and investment remain resilient, and markets are not clearly betting on rapid rate cuts.

Meanwhile, US crude inventories fell to dangerously low levels, the yen gained about 3% in a single day amid intervention rumors, and a weaker dollar pushed the renminbi to its highest level since early 2023.

**Today in one sentence:**

AI is moving from a “capabilities race” into a contest over efficiency, security, and the cost of capital; beyond AI, however, war-driven energy shocks and uncertainty over central-bank policy remain the biggest variables affecting markets.
