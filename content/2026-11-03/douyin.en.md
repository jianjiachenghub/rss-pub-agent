The AI boom now comes with an interest bill.

Today’s key takeaway: AI expansion is entering a phase where returns must prove themselves. All that spending needs revenue to back it up.

Meta has already raised $15 billion through bond sales. Higher bond yields have attracted investors, while also pushing up borrowing costs. As expansion continues, whether cash flow can cover interest payments will matter more and more.

How much those investments in computing power deliver also depends on data transfer. The deal between Marvell and Lumentum highlights the importance of optical networking: the bigger the cluster, the more its usable computing power depends on how efficiently data moves between chips and racks.

There’s also a signal to watch on the revenue side: Palantir’s latest earnings outlook suggests that sales growth driven by AI demand could slow.

Put it all together, and three questions will matter when assessing AI companies: How high are their borrowing costs, how efficiently does their computing infrastructure run, and how quickly can their investment turn into revenue?
