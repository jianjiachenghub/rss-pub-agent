**Nov. 3 Daily Brief｜Where Money Is Flowing, Where Risks Lie**

Today’s key stories connect technology, jobs, financial security, and international policy 👇

1️⃣ **Technology: AI Expansion Needs Both Funding and Bandwidth**

Oracle is raising $20 billion to expand its AI business; Meta has moved from considering borrowing to completing a $15 billion bond sale.

The deal between Marvell and Lumentum puts optical interconnects in the spotlight: the larger the cluster, the more efficiently data needs to move between chips and racks. Assessing AI infrastructure means looking at financing costs, network bandwidth, and project cash returns together.

2️⃣ **Security: Recruitment Can Also Be an Entry Point for Attackers**

According to Bloomberg, the Binance hack involved fake job interviews. The summary did not disclose the amount stolen.

Security incidents, alongside uncertainty over the U.S. midterm elections, coincided with more than $1 billion in net redemptions from U.S.-listed Bitcoin funds over two days. That figure represents money flowing out; the next question is whether redemptions continue.

3️⃣ **Macro: Expectations for Rapid Rate Cuts Face a Test**

U.S. job openings rose to a four-month high in September, while layoffs fell to their lowest level in over a year, suggesting labor demand is stabilizing.

If employment and wages remain resilient, markets may reassess their rate-cut expectations. Rate hikes are also expected from the UK and Swedish central banks, though official decisions are still pending. The path of interest rates will affect mortgages, corporate borrowing, and stock valuations.

4️⃣ **China Markets: Watch Debt Pressures and the Pace of IPOs**

Pressure surrounding Huafa Group’s $62 billion debt load has drawn attention in credit markets, though the repayment schedule remains unclear.

Separately, the Financial Times reports that China has paused IPOs to support struggling markets. The summary did not specify which markets are affected, the scope of the pause, or its duration. Assessing the impact will require further policy details and updates on corporate refinancing.

5️⃣ **Corporate Earnings: Markets Are Taking a Closer Look at Growth**

Shopify’s revenue missed market expectations for the first time since 2022, sending its shares sharply lower. Palantir’s outlook, meanwhile, suggests that AI-driven sales growth could slow.

For readers, the next things to watch are when orders turn into revenue and whether growth can last.

6️⃣ **Regulation: AI Compliance Planning May Need to Start Earlier**

China plans to introduce safety standards covering the entire AI lifecycle, from development to deployment. Training evaluations, release reviews, and monitoring after launch could all affect business costs and product timelines.

The standards are still planned; details on who they cover, when they take effect, and how responsibilities are assigned remain pending.

7️⃣ **International: Distinguish Policy Statements from Implementation**

According to the Financial Times, Trump said he would end military aid to Israel before year-end. The implementation plan remains unclear.

In Brazil, economists raised their medium- and long-term interest rate and inflation forecasts following Lula’s reelection. Future fiscal measures and the central bank’s response will shape whether this market repricing continues.
