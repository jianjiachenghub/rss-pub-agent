**11.21｜Markets Cool as the Rules for Compute Change**

Three themes to watch today: how global capital is seeking safety, where interest rates are headed, and how policy and costs are reshaping compute.

🌍 **Global Markets: Volatility Is Spreading Across Assets**

According to The Wall Street Journal, roughly $4 trillion in market value has been wiped out across Wall Street’s AI sector, with Anthropic giving back most of its early post-IPO gains.

On the same day, Bitcoin fell to its lowest level in more than two years; safe-haven demand for the yen and Swiss franc rose, while the dollar weakened. The VIX also reached its highest level since the 2025 tariff turmoil, and Taiwan expanded short-selling restrictions.

The key point: stocks, crypto, and currencies are all reflecting a shift in market sentiment.

🏦 **Interest Rates: Calls for Hikes Deserve Attention**

Fed official Paulson advocated planned interest rate hikes to curb inflation.

For anyone watching mortgages, corporate financing, or stock valuations, the path of interest rates remains a key variable. Still, this is one official’s view and should not be treated as a Fed decision to raise rates.

🧭 **Industrial Policy: Another Factor in Data Center Location Decisions**

Trump said he plans to penalize US companies that build data centers overseas. Future location decisions may need to weigh policy costs alongside electricity prices, land, and demand.

Meanwhile, reports suggest that new US AI chip rules targeting China could affect the distribution of compute capacity across Southeast Asia. The former remains a plan, while the latter’s actual impact will depend on the specific rules.

🛠️ **Technology in Practice: Progress on Chip Supply and Inference Efficiency**

Alibaba disclosed that its Zhenwu V900 is scheduled to enter mass production and go on sale in the first quarter of 2027, offering three times the compute performance of its predecessor and 216GB of GPU memory. Actual deliveries, software compatibility, and cluster efficiency will be the key things to watch.

AWS has introduced a distributed KV caching capability that reduces computation by reusing repeated context. The company says it can cut inference costs by up to 40% and time to first token by up to 80%; actual gains depend on cache hit rates and service overhead.

🔐 **Model Protection: Full Reasoning Traces Are Getting Harder to Extract**

OpenAI says the success rate for extracting GPT-6.2 Astra’s chain of thought fell from 54% to 0.2% in internal tests.

This could raise the cost of obtaining distillation data. Keep the scope of those figures in mind: they measure extraction success in internal tests and do not establish that all output imitation or capability transfer has been blocked.

🤝 **International Governance: Details on Military AI Limits Are Still Pending**

Bessent said US and Chinese leaders have agreed to limit the use of AI in military operations.

The next things to watch are the scope of those limits and how they will be enforced; the available summary has yet to disclose these details.
