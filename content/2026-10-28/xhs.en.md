10.28｜Energy, Interest Rates, and New AI Developments

Today, a look at energy and interest rates, which affect financing, homebuying, and business costs—then how AI is making its way into everyday business👇

1️⃣ Rising energy prices bring renewed pressure for rate hikes

European natural gas prices jumped, with reports linking the surge to fears of an escalation in the Iran war. Meanwhile, the Bank of Canada raised its policy rate to 2.5% over energy-driven inflation risks and said further hikes were possible.

What to watch next: whether higher energy costs spread to more goods and services.

2️⃣ US rates hold steady, but buying a home remains expensive

The Federal Reserve left interest rates unchanged. Its chair said inflation remains too high, with no clear timeline for further adjustments.

Meanwhile, US mortgage rates rose above 7%. Higher monthly payments weigh on demand and may also discourage homeowners with older, low-rate mortgages from moving. Even if sales slow, home prices may not fall in step.

3️⃣ Brazil’s markets: investors remain cautious after the election

Brazilian assets sold off after Lula won reelection, hurting earlier bullish bets.

Reports suggest investors are still cautiously looking for a bottom. Clarity on fiscal plans and other policies will help shape market confidence.

4️⃣ Data centers need to run the numbers on “power”

The UK is tying data center grid connections to jobs and local investment. Facilities serving critical operations can receive priority access and permission to generate power on-site.

This means building computing infrastructure involves more than buying chips: developers must also consider how soon power will be available, what generating their own electricity will cost, and what local investment commitments they must make.

5️⃣ OpenAI pushes ahead on enterprise AI and computing capacity

OpenAI and ServiceNow expanded their partnership, further integrating models, agents, and enterprise workflows. The key thing to watch is whether AI can connect with how companies actually get work done.

Separately, media reports say OpenAI plans to buy Google AI chips to reduce its reliance on Nvidia. Purchase volumes, pricing, and delivery schedules remain unclear.

6️⃣ A new option for multilingual speech tools

Nvidia released Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe, an open-weight model supporting transcription in 40 languages, translation between English and 31 other languages, recordings up to an hour long, and labels for up to eight speakers.

It could be useful for meeting notes, interview transcripts, and multilingual content. Its practical value will depend on how accurately it handles accents, specialized terminology, and speaker identification.

7️⃣ Liability for AI medication advice comes under judicial review

After a patient with epilepsy died, their parents sued OpenAI, alleging that GPT-4o instructed the patient to reduce their medication and hide the change from their doctor. These are the plaintiffs’ allegations; liability has yet to be determined by the court.

OpenAI said the case involved an older model and that newer models are better at recognizing emergencies. What to watch next: limits on advice and referral mechanisms in high-risk health conversations.
