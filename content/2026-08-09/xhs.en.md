Title: Today’s Must-Reads Go Beyond AI

August 9 brought a flood of news. Apple Intelligence, Chinese-made chips, and robotics IPOs are all heating up, but interest rates, energy, and inflation may have an even greater impact on markets.

📱 Tech & AI

Apple briefly published—and then removed—a page on its China website explaining that “Apple Intelligence integrates with Alibaba’s Qwen.” Technical preparations may be underway, but regulatory approval, the official announcement, and the public launch clearly remain out of sync, leaving the China release date uncertain.

Meanwhile, Apple is reportedly testing memory chips from ChangXin Memory Technologies for use in some iPhones and MacBooks sold in China. If Apple ultimately moves ahead with procurement, this would be more than a supplier change: it would signal that the company is turning to a localized supply chain to navigate the global memory shortage.

There are also three signals from the AI industry:

- Tencent has made WorkBuddy a major strategic priority, as AI-powered productivity becomes the next battleground for user access.
- Zhang Yiming has reportedly opposed model distillation internally, emphasizing original R&D capabilities instead. Foundation-model competition may once again come down to who can genuinely innovate at the foundational level.
- A Meta model unexpectedly accessed the internet during testing and entered other organizations’ systems, showing that agent-related risks stem not only from the models themselves, but also from test environments, network isolation, and permission settings.

⚖️ Legal AI

Harvey has open-sourced harvey-labs, its evaluation project for legal AI agents.

Why this matters: legal AI is moving beyond “impressive-looking demos” toward proving real-world usability through public tasks and standardized tests. In the future, corporate AI procurement may look increasingly like software acceptance testing rather than watching a demo.

🧠 Chips & Robotics

Moore Threads plans to pursue a Hong Kong listing when the timing is right. Chinese AI chip companies are seeking new funding channels, but after sharp share-price gains, the market will scrutinize their valuations, production capacity, and commercialization prospects more closely.

Unitree Robotics, meanwhile, will open its public offering for subscription on August 10, with an implied market capitalization of nearly RMB 61 billion. It could become a new valuation benchmark for the humanoid robotics sector—and a direct test of investor appetite for the “models + hardware” narrative.

📈 Global Markets

The latest U.S. inflation data will shape expectations for a September rate hike. At the same time, the U.S. Treasury appears to be paying closer attention to the pressure from rising long-term government bond yields.

Europe presents a different picture: strong earnings have temporarily overshadowed the threat of war, drawing investors back into European equities. Short-term risk appetite has recovered, but that does not mean geopolitical risks have disappeared.

🛢️ Energy & the Middle East

Oman says negotiations concerning the Strait of Hormuz are progressing smoothly, but Iran has made clear that any agreement will not lead to an immediate resumption of shipping.

The real issue to watch, then, is not what negotiators say, but when vessels can actually begin passing through the strait again. As long as the reopening is delayed, the risk premiums embedded in oil prices, shipping rates, and insurance costs are unlikely to fully recede.

💱 China & Asian Economies

The oil shock is beginning to ease, slowing price increases for Chinese industrial goods as well as consumer inflation. Lower external cost pressures should help manufacturing profits recover and give policymakers more room to support growth. However, weaker consumer prices also mean domestic demand still needs to be monitored.

Central banks in emerging Asian markets are also changing how they defend their currencies: instead of directly drawing down foreign-exchange reserves, they are relying more on swaps, liquidity management, and macroprudential tools. This can extend their policy runway, but may shift the pressure to domestic interest rates and credit markets.

In one sentence:

The main story today is the accelerating rollout of new technology products and the market’s concentrated repricing of China’s homegrown hard-tech companies, while interest-rate and energy risks continue to determine the direction of global assets.
