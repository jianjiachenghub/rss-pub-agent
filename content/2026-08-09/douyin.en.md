The biggest story today isn’t a few more AI products. It’s that the tech race is entering three tough phases at once: regulatory approval, supply-chain restructuring, and capital-market pricing.

Apple is the clearest example. Its China website briefly published instructions for integrating Apple Intelligence with Alibaba’s Qwen, then removed them. That suggests the technology may be ready, but regulatory review, official announcements, and the formal rollout are not yet aligned. Apple Intelligence’s China launch remains uncertain.

Meanwhile, Apple is testing memory chips from China’s CXMT for use in some iPhones and MacBooks sold in China. If Apple moves forward with procurement, it would give Chinese memory chips a chance to prove themselves in top-tier consumer electronics—and help Apple use local suppliers to hedge against the global memory shortage.

Capital markets are also repricing hard tech. Unitree Robotics opened for subscription today at a valuation of nearly 61 billion yuan, with institutional demand exceeding 2,600 times the shares available. Moore Threads is also planning a Hong Kong listing.

So today’s key takeaway is this: competition in AI and hard tech is moving beyond “who tells the better story” to who can actually clear regulatory hurdles, enter major supply chains, and withstand the public market’s valuation test.
