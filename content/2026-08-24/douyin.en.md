Today’s key takeaway: AI competition has moved beyond “who has the best model” to “who controls the chips, the capital, and the gateway to users.”

Nvidia plans to invest $6 billion to support America’s open AI ecosystem—while raising prices for its next-generation AI servers by more than 15%. That shows it doesn’t just want to sell chips. It wants to lock in models, developers, and applications—and turn scarce computing power into even greater pricing power.

The tech giants are also racing to secure their positions. Alibaba plans to raise HK$80 billion through a share placement, investing all of it in AI. ByteDance, meanwhile, is integrating TRAE, Coze, and productivity tools into Doubao to compete for a unified gateway to getting tasks done.

But behind the boom, cost risks are already emerging: one developer spent $1,386 in just four days after connecting to a model—and an astonishing 85% went to cache writes. Microsoft’s AI business is also heavily concentrated among a handful of major customers.

So when evaluating AI companies going forward, don’t just look at model rankings. Focus on three things: where the money comes from, who controls the gateway, and whether every unit of computing power can actually earn back its cost.
