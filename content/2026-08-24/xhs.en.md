Title: Today, There’s More to Watch Than Rising AI Prices

The key developments from August 24 can be summarized in five major themes:

① AI is entering a capital-intensive race

Nvidia plans to invest $6 billion in building an open AI ecosystem in the U.S., Alibaba plans to raise around HK$80 billion through a share placement, SoftBank is preparing to issue ¥1 trillion in retail bonds, and XPeng’s robotics business is also seeking $900 million in funding.

The signal is clear: AI competition is no longer just about model capabilities. It is now a broader contest involving capital, computing power, developer ecosystems, and access to users.

② Computing power is getting more expensive, bringing costs into focus

Nvidia has informed customers that prices for some next-generation AI servers will rise by more than 15%. Meanwhile, a developer who accessed Codex CLI through Bedrock spent about $1,386 in four days, with 85% going toward prompt cache writes.

Both large companies and individual developers need to reassess how they procure computing resources, manage caching, and price inference. Whether AI can make money increasingly depends on cost control.

③ AI products are competing to become the “super app”

ByteDance is integrating capabilities from TRAE, Coze, and other products into Doubao, while planning to launch a unified workplace brand called “Doubao Work.” ChatGPT, meanwhile, has begun using Computer History to understand users’ activity across applications.

The next generation of AI assistants may no longer be just chatboxes, but comprehensive gateways connecting coding, office work, search, and task execution. At the same time, transparent permissions, privacy governance, and enterprise auditing will become increasingly important.

④ Tariffs and geopolitical risks are feeding into prices and markets

The U.S. plans to raise tariffs on Canadian automobiles and steel and is considering additional tariffs on some Chinese products. The news pushed the Canadian dollar lower and Canadian bonds higher as markets began pricing in weaker exports and slower growth.

Meanwhile, the cost of transporting crude oil through the Strait of Hormuz has reportedly risen to $20 million. Higher tariffs, insurance, security, and freight costs may ultimately flow into business expenses and consumer prices.

⑤ Climate risks are also reshaping the economic equation

As a “super El Niño” develops, global ocean temperatures have reached their highest levels on record. U.S. AI data centers are also facing pressure from storms, power outages, and rising insurance costs.

This means climate change is no longer just an environmental story—it directly affects energy, logistics, infrastructure, and computing costs.

The one sentence worth remembering today:

The AI boom is still going strong, but the market’s focus has shifted from “Who has the strongest model?” to “Who can bear the higher costs of capital, energy, and supply chains?”
