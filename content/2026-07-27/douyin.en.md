Stop obsessing over AI model rankings. The biggest shift today is this: AI competition is evolving from “who has the best model” into a full-scale restructuring of capital, energy, and employment.

The clearest signal comes from Nvidia. It is reportedly discussing guarantees of up to $250 billion for OpenAI’s data centers, while advancing more than $750 billion in AI-related deals. But the key question is: Is this demand backed by real cash flow—or by a capital loop connecting chip sales, investments, and customer purchases? The answer will directly determine whether AI valuations can hold.

Second, AI isn’t simply wiping out jobs. Giants in tech, transportation, and defense are hiring again, while OpenAI research shows that AI is helping employees move beyond the traditional boundaries of their roles. The next priority for companies may not be how many people to cut, but how to retrain workers and redesign tasks so humans and AI can boost output together.

Third, the macro environment is getting more dangerous. Volatile oil prices are increasing inflation pressure. The Federal Reserve may not only delay rate cuts—the market may even need to reassess the risk of rate hikes.

In one sentence: AI is still expanding at breakneck speed, but the next phase won’t be decided by model capabilities alone. The real winners will be those with cheaper capital, stable energy, and people who truly know how to use AI.
