# Today’s Must-Reads: AI Spending, Hiring Rebounds, and Shifting Interest Rates

July 27 brought a flood of news. AI remains the central theme, but what truly matters is that it is now reshaping financing, employment, semiconductors, energy, and policy all at once.

💰 **1｜AI Enters the “Mega-Financing” Era**

Nvidia is reportedly discussing guarantees of up to $250 billion for OpenAI’s data center projects while pursuing more than $750 billion in AI-related deals.

The question is not just how much is being invested, but whether chip sales, investments, and customer purchases could form a circular flow of capital. To judge whether the AI boom is healthy, we need to look for genuine cash flow behind the demand.

👩‍💻 **2｜Large Companies Are Hiring Again, and AI Hasn’t Eliminated Jobs—Yet**

Major technology, transportation, and defense companies are ending hiring freezes that lasted roughly a year and are recruiting again. OpenAI research also suggests that AI is helping employees work beyond the boundaries of their traditional roles.

Rather than jobs disappearing en masse, work itself is more likely to be reorganized. The ability to collaborate with AI and handle cross-functional responsibilities is becoming increasingly important.

📈 **3｜Global Interest Rate Expectations Are Growing Uncertain Again**

Inflation risks and volatile oil prices have brought the tail risk of another Federal Reserve rate hike back into view. The European Central Bank may also continue raising rates in September. Meanwhile, the Swiss National Bank could keep rates at zero through the end of 2027.

Global monetary policies are clearly diverging, making bonds, stocks, and currencies more difficult to price.

🇨🇳 **4｜China Is Rapidly Strengthening Its Technology Foundations**

Several pieces of Chinese technology news today are worth viewing together:

- China is planning “six major networks” of infrastructure for the AI era
- Moonshot AI released a new model and made it available for download
- CXMT is seeking to raise $8.55 billion through an IPO, betting on domestically produced memory chips
- Reports of progress in Chinese-made DUV equipment sent ASML shares lower

Competition is no longer confined to AI models. It now extends to computing power, electricity, networks, storage, and lithography equipment.

🏭 **5｜AI Demand Continues to Support Chinese Manufacturing**

Growth in profits at China’s industrial companies slowed slightly in June, but global AI demand continues to boost profits and exports in related manufacturing sectors. The AI boom is spreading from internet companies to equipment makers, chipmakers, and industrial supply chains.

🏛️ **6｜Policy Is Playing a Growing Role in Directing Capital**

AI companies’ lobbying spending in Washington has reached a record high, while economies including the United States and Japan are actively steering private capital toward priority industries. At the same time, China is tightening compliance requirements for offshore investment and corporate structures.

Capital allocation is no longer driven solely by returns. Regulation, industrial policy, and national strategy increasingly matter too.

🌍 **7｜Don’t Overlook Two “Macro Gray Rhinos”**

Argentina is approaching a $57 billion debt crunch. Whether the IMF adjusts its repayment arrangements will directly affect how much room President Milei has to pursue his reforms.

Oil prices have also swung sharply, but turbulence in shipping markets suggests the sell-off may not last. Energy prices could still feed back into inflation and interest rates.

In one sentence: **Today’s most important shift is that AI has evolved from a “model race” into a broad competition over capital, infrastructure, employment, and policy rules.**
