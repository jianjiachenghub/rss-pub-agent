AI competition is shifting from “who has the strongest model” to “who can secure the cheapest capital, computing power, and users.”

The clearest signal: Anthropic leads in model performance, but its higher prices are putting pressure on user growth. Meanwhile, Nvidia is preparing to commit around $7 billion—through licensing, investment, and talent recruitment—to deepen its ties with Poolside, expanding beyond chip sales into model-building tools.

But computing power is also getting more expensive. As memory costs rise, prices for some Nvidia AI servers could jump by more than 15%. In the U.S., tens of billions of dollars in debt are funding data center construction—even high-risk credit is pouring into AI bonds. Alibaba is also planning a $10.2 billion share placement to keep financing its AI investments.

So today’s key takeaway is this: AI has entered a capital-intensive era. The winners won’t be decided by model parameters alone, but by who can secure cheaper financing, build a more resilient supply chain, and turn expensive computing power into recurring revenue.

The technology story continues—but financial risk has already joined the table.
