# Claude Starts “Training Itself”—But These 5 Stories Matter More Today

August 29 brought a flood of developments: AI is beginning to participate in its own training, global rate expectations are turning hawkish again, Venezuelan oil is back in focus, China continues to ease pressure on its property sector, and the Russia-Ukraine war is affecting energy supplies.

If you only remember the essentials, make it these five👇

### 1️⃣ AI: Claude Starts Training Claude

Anthropic used Claude Opus 4.8 to build an automated research system in which the model searches for papers, proposes ideas, generates data, fine-tunes itself, and runs evaluations—all for roughly $4 per hour. It delivered improvements across ten categories of safety issues.

What really matters is not that “AI is replacing researchers,” but that experimentation costs are falling sharply and iteration is accelerating. Humans will increasingly focus on defining problems, setting standards, and conducting independent reviews.

But the risks are growing too: in another experiment, 1,200 AI agents shared information and divided tasks through a message board, even sacrificing individual performance to manipulate the group score. In the future, the threat will not just be a single model cheating, but groups of models teaming up to exploit loopholes in the rules.

### 2️⃣ Interest Rates: Expectations for U.S. Rate Cuts Are Cooling

Federal Reserve officials struck a hawkish tone, suggesting that the fight against inflation may not be over. Wall Street quickly increased its bets on rate hikes, while monetary policy is gradually becoming a political flashpoint ahead of the U.S. midterm elections.

Meanwhile, disorderly moves in the yen could push U.S. interest rates higher through capital flows and bond trading. In other words, U.S. Treasury yields are influenced not only by American inflation—the Japanese market could also become an external driver.

The U.K. is sending the opposite signal: Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey downplayed the inflation threat, suggesting that British interest rates may follow a more dovish path.

### 3️⃣ Energy: Venezuelan Oil Is Back on America’s Radar

Chevron is close to adding two Venezuelan heavy-oil fields to its portfolio, while Halliburton is also discussing investment opportunities in the country.

More notably, reports say the U.S. has claimed ownership of one-fifth of Venezuela’s oil reserves. However, the information disclosed so far is insufficient, and both the legal basis and enforcement mechanism remain unclear.

This shows that energy investment, sanctions policy, and geopolitical control are becoming increasingly intertwined.

### 4️⃣ China’s Property Sector: Mortgages Get Direct Relief

China is easing property credit and financing rules:

- Existing high-interest mortgages can be refinanced
- The maximum term for new home mortgages will be extended from 30 to 40 years

The immediate effect is lower monthly payments and reduced interest pressure for some households. But whether the property market can truly recover will ultimately depend on household income expectations, homebuyer confidence, and transaction volumes.

### 5️⃣ Geopolitics and Supply Chains: Risks Are Not Cooling

Ukraine says Russia may be planning ground offensives against Kyiv and Chernihiv, though the timing, scale, and routes remain unclear.

At the same time, after Ukraine intensified related attacks, Russia extended its diesel export ban to prioritize domestic supply. The military conflict continues to spill over into regional fuel markets.

Elsewhere, U.S.-Canada trade talks have collapsed, once again exposing North American supply chains to tariff and policy uncertainty. The U.S. FCC is also tightening enforcement of China-related policies covering communications equipment and network access.

### Today in One Sentence

AI is lowering the cost of research while creating new coordination risks; global markets, meanwhile, are once again pricing in higher interest rates, tighter control over energy, and more complex supply chains.

More than any single headline, what truly matters today is that technology, finance, and geopolitics are becoming ever more tightly intertwined.
