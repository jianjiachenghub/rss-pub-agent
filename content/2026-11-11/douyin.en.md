The AI boom is starting to count the full cost.

Today’s key takeaway: whether AI spending can continue depends on whether financing keeps flowing—and whether applications actually save money.

According to the Financial Times, banks are hitting their AI lending limits and turning to credit insurance to keep financing flowing. Expanding computing capacity means finding more capital willing to take on the risk.

AI applications are being judged on efficiency, too. OpenAI’s Codex security tool links vulnerability detection, verification, and fixes, potentially cutting manual work. shadcn’s improve project uses powerful models to review code and develop a plan, then hands execution to lower-cost models to try to cut development costs.

But cheaper tools don’t always make the whole job cheaper. False positives need review, fixes need verification, and execution errors mean rework.

So, to judge AI’s value from here, watch two things: whether financing can keep flowing, and how much money it actually saves getting the whole job done.
