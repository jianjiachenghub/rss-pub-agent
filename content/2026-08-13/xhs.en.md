# Today’s Must-Read: As AI Races Ahead, Power and Money Are Already Running Tight

August 13 brought a flood of news, but what truly deserves attention is not just “another new model launch.” AI is simultaneously reshaping technology, capital, energy, and regulation.

### 1️⃣ The Model Race: Open Source Reaches Max-Level Performance for the First Time

Alibaba has open-sourced Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B, featuring 2.4 trillion total parameters and 95 billion active parameters, and touts it as the most powerful open-source model to date.

Meanwhile, DeepSeek V4 Pro has officially launched across its app, web platform, and API, while Grok 4.6 has earned positive reviews for its agent capabilities and cost efficiency.

The focus of model competition has shifted. It is no longer just about benchmark scores, but also about whether agents can actually complete tasks and how much each output costs.

Even more strikingly, a neurosurgery resident at Peking Union Medical College Hospital reportedly used GPT-5.6 in a long-running autonomous process to prove the Crouzeix conjecture, which had challenged mathematicians for 22 years. The proof was subsequently verified by experts. Whether AI is beginning to participate in substantive knowledge discovery is a development worth watching closely.

### 2️⃣ The User War: One Billion Monthly Active Users Is the New Threshold

Google announced that Gemini has surpassed one billion monthly active users, making it the fastest-growing product in Google’s history.

This means AI products can no longer be judged solely by model rankings. What matters next is whether users keep coming back, whether search habits shift, and whether traffic can genuinely be converted into revenue.

Apple is also discussing multi-year paid agreements with publishers, hoping to improve its AI-powered Siri with real-time news and high-quality information. Trusted, timely, copyrighted content may become one of the most important assets in the battle to control AI gateways.

### 3️⃣ The Capital War: AI Is Burning Through Money

Anthropic is reportedly in talks to acquire AI startup Decart for around $6 billion; AMD plans to issue $4.75 billion in debt; and Wall Street is actively underwriting bond and stock offerings from AI companies.

Tencent reported negative free cash flow of RMB 13.8 billion in the second quarter, also partly due to advance payments for computing capacity. But this does not necessarily mean its operations suddenly deteriorated. It looks more like future computing costs being paid upfront.

The key question is: How long will it take for these enormous investments to translate into revenue and cash flow?

### 4️⃣ The Energy War: Electricity Is Becoming AI’s Real Ceiling

Texas is cooling on AI data centers, while India’s power grid is simultaneously grappling with rising electricity demand, extreme weather, and the added load from new data centers.

Even as Microsoft ramps up its AI investments, it has cut carbon-removal purchases by 80%, further exposing the tension between expanding computing capacity and honoring climate commitments.

The bottlenecks to scaling AI may not be model parameters, but power grids, land, regulatory approvals, and carbon costs.

### 5️⃣ Chips: Capital Keeps Flowing Toward Infrastructure

SMIC reported year-over-year growth in both revenue and profit for the second quarter, benefiting from demand for conventional and specialty semiconductors, as well as spillover orders caused by tight global AI production capacity.

There are even market rumors that memory-chip maker CXMT has surpassed Tencent in valuation. The ranking itself should not be taken at face value—the valuation methodology still needs to be confirmed—but the signal is clear: capital is repricing chips and computing infrastructure.

### 6️⃣ Macro Markets: Interest Rates, Energy, and Capital Flows Are All Shifting

U.S. traders have reduced their bets on Federal Reserve rate hikes, but Fed officials remain concerned that inflation may no longer be steadily cooling. The path of interest rates is far from settled.

In Japan, pressure on the yen has prompted calls for the central bank to accelerate rate hikes. Foreign capital is exiting Brazil’s stock market at the fastest pace since 2021, while concerns over European natural gas prices and storage levels are heating up again.

After a wave of speculative trading in individual stocks, South Korea is also requiring new investors to undergo training. Meanwhile, as retail participation and liquidity recede, the crypto market is moving further into an institution-dominated “Wall Street era.”

### 7️⃣ Security and Regulation: AI Is Entering the Centers of Power

Taiwan has faced a cyber incident described as an “autonomous AI hacking attack,” while U.S. trade enforcement officials have begun using AI to identify tariff evasion.

At the same time, research suggests that ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini may exhibit systematic output biases on certain sensitive China-related issues. When procuring AI systems, companies should not limit themselves to performance testing. They also need to examine regional differences, moderation boundaries, and whether answer sources are traceable.

Demis Hassabis previously proposed establishing an independent AI industry safety body, another sign that leading companies are actively helping shape regulatory rules.

📌 Today’s most important takeaway:

AI is moving beyond the “model upgrade story” and entering a stage defined by real-world constraints.

On one side, open-source foundation models, billion-user products, and agent capabilities are advancing rapidly. On the other, power shortages, capital expenditure, cash-flow pressure, content copyright, and safety regulation are all emerging at once.

The winners may not be those with the largest models, but those that can secure reliable computing power, real users, high-quality content, and sustainable revenue at a lower cost.
