Today’s most important signal isn’t which new model just launched. It’s that the AI race has evolved from a battle of models into a battle for capital, energy, and access to users.

On one side, Alibaba has brought Max-level capabilities into the open-source ecosystem for the first time, launching Qwen3.8 with 2.4 trillion total parameters. DeepSeek has also released the official version of V4 Pro. Model capabilities keep spreading, and technical barriers keep falling.

But on the other side, the real bottleneck is shifting to infrastructure. Texas is cooling on AI data centers. India’s power grid is straining under new demand. Microsoft has even cut its carbon-removal purchases by 80%. The expansion of computing capacity is now directly competing for electricity, land, and environmental resources.

Capital is also doubling down: Anthropic is in talks for an acquisition worth roughly $6 billion, while AMD plans to issue $4.75 billion in debt. Meanwhile, Gemini has surpassed one billion monthly active users—showing that industry valuations are shifting away from benchmark scores and toward user scale, cost per task, and monetization efficiency.

So when evaluating AI companies from here on, don’t just look at model rankings. The likely winners of the next phase will be those that secure cheaper compute, more reliable energy, and users they can actually monetize.
