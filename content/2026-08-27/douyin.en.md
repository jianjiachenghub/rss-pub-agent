The biggest signal in AI today isn’t that models are getting stronger again. It’s that tech giants are fighting to control the entire industry chain—from chips and models to cloud platforms.

Nvidia is reportedly in talks to acquire Hugging Face for $13 billion. It’s not just buying a company—it’s buying a key distribution gateway for open-source models. With sales doubling to $96 billion and roughly 70% growth expected next year, Nvidia is extending its computing advantage into ecosystem control.

The second signal: capital spending is still soaring. Anthropic has signed $45 billion worth of data center partnerships, while SK Hynix, Kioxia, and SanDisk plan to invest tens of billions of dollars to expand advanced memory and flash capacity. The AI race has moved beyond model parameters. It’s now a capital-intensive battle over power, chips, and storage.

But there’s another side to the story. Tokens are getting cheaper, yet businesses still haven’t fully proven the returns. Alphabet’s sharp decline in market value shows that investors are starting to ask: after spending all this money, where are the profits?

So today’s takeaway is this: the AI boom isn’t cooling down, but the focus of competition has shifted—from “who has the strongest model” to “who controls distribution, infrastructure, and revenue sharing.” The most valuable asset going forward may not be any single technology, but a complete ecosystem.
