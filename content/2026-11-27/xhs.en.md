Nov. 27 Daily Brief｜Stocks surge, AI compute gets harder to access

There’s more to watch today than AI: a stock market rebound, European interest rates, and shifts in hiring. Here are six key themes to help you quickly grasp the implications👇

① China’s stock market: What to watch after the surge?

After midday statements signaling support for market stability, the Shanghai Composite rose 3.7%, the Shenzhen Composite 7.2%, and the ChiNext Index 11.6%, with tech and growth stocks posting stronger rebounds.

Next, watch whether support policies include clear funding commitments, and whether trading volumes and earnings expectations follow through. A one-day surge does not establish that money will keep flowing in.

② AI compute: Renting GPUs overseas faces new restrictions too

The U.S. has extended restrictions on Chinese companies from obtaining chips to remotely using Nvidia chips overseas. The affected computing capacity is mainly located in Malaysia.

Nvidia subsequently asked partners to pause shipments to Southeast Asia; Singapore is in talks with the U.S., with no exemption announced so far.

For AI teams, budgets need to account for several questions: whether compute will be accessible, how to adapt to alternative chips, and when equipment will arrive.

③ Overseas assets: Stocks and Bitcoin diverge

U.S. stocks rebounded, while Bitcoin fell below $60,000 again. Assessing funding needs in crypto requires a separate look at the market’s liquidity and leverage.

Meanwhile, South Korea’s National Pension Service saw its assets shrink by about $151.4 billion in the third quarter, highlighting the drawdown risk from equity exposure. More disclosure is needed to determine whether this involved realized losses or pressure on pension payments.

④ European interest rates: Signals of further tightening

ECB policymaker Nagel suggested that stubborn inflation could require another rate hike in December, though this is just one official’s view.

According to the Financial Times, the Bank of England still intends to tighten gradually, at a pace that differs from market expectations. The size of any moves remains unclear.

For readers tracking financing costs, bonds, and exchange rates, the next things to watch are inflation data and official policy decisions.

⑤ AI and the workplace: Tool budgets and hiring are shifting

After OpenAI corrected its usage accounting, users now hit their limits 40% faster on GPT-6.2 Astra and 25% faster on Sol. The change concerns how usage is counted, so regular users need to reassess how many tasks they can complete each month. Past undercounted usage will not be deducted retroactively, and four paid plan tiers received a one-time usage reset that can be saved for later.

Microsoft, meanwhile, is slowing graduate hiring as it prioritizes internal staffing adjustments and AI budget commitments. One question to keep watching: as companies spend more on compute, how will entry-level roles and career development paths change?

⑥ Defense procurement: AI enters major contracts

Poland signed a $300 million agreement with Palantir to use AI software to analyze battlefield data.

The deal shows that defense AI is now part of concrete procurement budgets. Beyond contract value, control over data and long-term dependence on suppliers also shape European countries’ choices.
