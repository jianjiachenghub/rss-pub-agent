# Personal Daily Report | November 27, 2026

## Today's Take

> Today's central view is that policy is shaping both technology supply and asset pricing: computing restrictions add uncertainty to AI expansion, while market support signals lift near-term risk appetite. Sustained gains still need confirmation from capital flows. Chip availability and model usage costs must be assessed alongside capability improvements; AI products and developer tools also need to be reassessed based on actual quota usage and engineering efficiency.

## AI

### US closes loophole allowing remote access to overseas Nvidia chips

**Event:** The US has updated export controls to close a loophole allowing Chinese companies to remotely use Nvidia chips overseas, primarily in Malaysia. The restrictions now extend from hardware acquisition to remote use.

**Why it matters:** Compliance requirements now cover actual users as well as chip locations, reducing access to computing capacity deployed overseas. Chinese companies budgeting for training must account for remote access eligibility, adaptation to alternative chips and supply disruption costs.

### Nvidia asks partners to pause chip shipments to Southeast Asia

**Event:** Following updated US export rules tightening restrictions on remote use of AI hardware, Nvidia has asked partners to pause chip shipments to Southeast Asia, putting regional customers' delivery schedules in flux.

**Why it matters:** Order fulfillment increasingly depends on export compliance, and ample chip inventory may still face delivery barriers. Southeast Asian data center projects need to reassess arrival dates; the scope of the pause and conditions for resuming shipments will determine the extent of delays.

Score 92 · Source [The Wall Street Journal](https://cn.wsj.com/articles/nvidia-tells-partners-to-pause-shipments-to-southeast-asia-919e1bc9)

### Singapore in talks with US to preserve access to advanced chips

**Event:** Singapore's Deputy Prime Minister Gan Kim Yong said the country is in talks with the US to maintain access to advanced semiconductors after Washington tightened chip export restrictions on Singapore.

**Why it matters:** The talks will affect the predictability of advanced chip supplies for Singapore's data centers and delivery timelines for local computing projects. The key questions are which chips can be supplied and under what conditions. Discussions remain ongoing, with no reported exemption secured.

### OpenAI corrects usage metering, accelerating quota consumption for advanced models

**Event:** OpenAI has corrected a GPT-6.2 usage metering error, causing Astra and Sol to reach their caps 40% and 25% faster, respectively. Past undercounted usage will not be deducted retroactively, and four paid plan tiers receive a one-time quota reset that can be saved for later.

**Why it matters:** Metering changes affect how many tasks a subscription can complete, requiring teams to recalibrate last week's usage budgets. Development teams can assign models according to task value. Other models are unaffected, and the one-time reset offers only temporary relief.

Score 89 · Source [Readhub - Daily Briefing - ITHome](https://readhub.cn/topic/8yNSeGheS5z)

### Microsoft slows graduate hiring as budgets shift toward AI

**Event:** Satya Nadella acknowledged that Microsoft has slowed graduate hiring, prioritizing internal staff redeployment, AI budgets and a “software factory” in which people and AI share tasks. Its workforce shrank for the first time in a decade while revenue grew.

**Why it matters:** Software development spending is shifting toward computing capacity and process redesign, with junior roles a key focus of staffing adjustments. Output per employee may rise in the short term, but a narrower training pipeline requires companies to reassess the future cost of developing experienced engineers.

Score 86 · Source [Readhub - Daily Briefing - PEdaily](https://readhub.cn/topic/8yObe4EBTX3)
