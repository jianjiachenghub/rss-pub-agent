It’s time to redo the math on AI! Today’s biggest takeaway: reliable access to compute and control over usage costs are becoming key to winning the AI race.

The U.S. has extended export controls from buying chips to using them remotely, tightening restrictions on Chinese companies renting compute overseas. Soon after, Nvidia told its partners to pause chip shipments to Southeast Asia. Companies budgeting for AI training now have to factor in rental fees, supply disruptions, delivery delays, and the cost of adapting to different chips.

Corporate budgets are shifting, too. Microsoft is slowing hiring of new graduates, prioritizing AI spending, and rethinking how people and machines divide software development work. That means compute spending is shaping staffing decisions.

Going forward, judge an AI company’s competitiveness by two things: whether it can secure reliable access to compute, and whether its investment delivers consistent results. With every expansion, companies need to get that math right.
