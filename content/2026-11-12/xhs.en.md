Nov. 12 Daily Brief | Cheaper Video, Rates Under Pressure

Today’s highlights include new pricing for creative tools, plus changes in interest rates, trade, and markets that could affect your wallet👇

🎬 ① AI video is getting cheaper—check the cost of a finished clip

Volcano Engine has launched Seedance 2.5 flash, claiming twice the generation speed and prices about 34% lower. At 720p, it costs roughly RMB 0.99 per second, with clips up to 30 seconds long. API access is scheduled for November 15.

Do the math: 30 seconds costs about RMB 29.70. Repeated attempts will push that budget higher. If you make short videos, watch how often it produces usable clips and how the image quality holds up.

🧩 ② The chip supply outlook is affecting cloud providers

The daily brief cites a Bloomberg report about a major leadership change at Nvidia involving Jensen Huang’s departure. The details still need to be checked against the original article and company announcements.

Alibaba shares fell 7% on the day, with reports linking the decline to concerns about access to advanced chips. For cloud businesses, chip procurement costs and delivery timelines both affect growth. However, the available material does not show that new supply restrictions have taken effect.

💵 ③ Inflation and interest rates continue to shape homebuying and investing

Reports say U.S. October CPI came in above expectations. Cleveland Fed President Hammack favors tighter interest rate policy and cited demand pressures from AI investment.

Meanwhile, U.S. existing home sales last month fell to their lowest level since 2010. Two indicators worth watching are mortgage rates and purchasing power. Weak sales alone are not enough to tell where home prices are heading.

There is also news from China’s bond market: the government is reportedly considering taxing interest on government bonds. The next thing to watch is how the specific proposal would affect returns after tax.

🚚 ④ Mexico tariff threats matter for cross-border business

The Financial Times reports that Trump has threatened to double tariffs on Mexico over illegal immigration.

For now, this remains a threat. Those working in cross-border trade, manufacturing, and supply chains should watch whether it is implemented, which goods it covers, and how businesses bear the costs.

📉 ⑤ Markets are scrutinizing the economics of AI

Nebius shares fell on a disappointing sales outlook, as investors focused more closely on whether spending on computing capacity can translate into revenue.

The Australian dollar fell to its lowest level since January, while Bitcoin pulled back. Other reports say some foreign investors are looking at Indian stocks to diversify away from AI risk. Together, these stories suggest investors are paying closer attention to whether growth materializes and how to spread risk. Whether this becomes a lasting trend remains to be seen.

🛠️ ⑥ A local inference project for developers to watch

TensorFold implements large language model inference in Zig, supporting Metal, CUDA, and Vulkan and adding another option for deployment across different GPUs.

Before trying it, check whether it can run your target model, how much GPU memory it uses, and its actual throughput. Repository popularity reflects interest; performance still needs to be measured in practice.
