# Today’s Must-Read: AI Is Surging—and Money and Chips Are Getting Tight

September 5 brought a flood of developments. The main story isn’t simply “another new model”—AI’s expansion is now having a tangible impact on chips, manufacturing, capital markets, and employment.

## 1｜AI Is Squeezing Consumer Electronics’ Chip Supply

AI servers are consuming vast amounts of memory capacity, and “RAMageddon” is beginning to affect consumer electronics such as smartphones and computers.

Foxconn’s sales rose 52% year over year, another sign that demand for computing power has translated from hype into manufacturing revenue. To ease the memory bottleneck, Kioxia plans to use CXL modules so that cheaper NAND can handle some workloads currently assigned to DRAM.

In short: the flip side of the AI boom may be higher hardware costs for consumers—and a redesign of server storage architecture.

## 2｜AI Competition Is Expanding From Model Capabilities to Ecosystem Control

Nvidia reportedly plans to acquire Hugging Face for about $12.9 billion. What matters isn’t simply the addition of another company, but the possibility that Nvidia could control GPUs, a key model distribution channel, and the developer ecosystem all at once.

OpenAI’s release of GPT-6 Astra is also about more than parameters. Long-term memory, tool use, software operation, and lower API costs are all pushing AI from “answering questions” toward “completing tasks autonomously.”

Meanwhile, Samsung and Arm are collaborating on 2nm chips for on-device AI, with OpenAI viewed as a potential customer. In the future, more inference workloads may shift from the cloud to smartphones and other devices.

## 3｜AI Is Beginning to Take On Massive Mathematical Projects

With Claude’s assistance, Peng Tianyi’s team generated 13 million lines of Lean code in 11 days, constructed 30,300 verifiable theorems, and completed an end-to-end formal proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem.

The point isn’t to rediscover Fermat’s Last Theorem, but that AI dramatically reduced the labor and time required for formal verification. This could accelerate the development of mathematical knowledge bases, highly reliable software, and security systems.

## 4｜As Valuations Soar, Risks Are Rising Too

Anthropic is reportedly preparing to go public at a $2 trillion valuation, pushing the valuation benchmark for AI companies even higher.

On the other hand, investment vehicles linked to DeepSeek have reportedly charged hefty upfront and backend fees while imposing long lockup periods, offering no governance rights, and even failing to guarantee ownership of genuine shares.

Anyone looking to invest in AI should examine more than headline projects and nominal valuations. The authenticity of the shares, fee structure, exit mechanisms, and investor rights all need careful verification.

## 5｜Global Markets Are Trading on “High Interest Rates + Geopolitical Risk”

Hawkish signals from Federal Reserve officials and a sell-off in U.S. Treasuries have driven up financing costs, putting the most vulnerable borrowers under pressure first. Meanwhile, a Jefferies fund has disclosed potential exposure to $500 million in suspected invoice fraud, once again highlighting due-diligence problems in private credit.

Tensions involving Iran have prompted hedge funds to increase their bullish bets on crude oil. But markets are also pricing in another scenario: once the conflict ends, oil prices could fall sharply, bringing inflation and bond yields down with them.

Interestingly, Wall Street risk assets and some emerging markets remain resilient. It’s not that the market sees no risks—it is betting that those risks remain manageable for now.

## 6｜AI Has Not Yet Triggered a “Jobs Apocalypse”

The more visible shift so far is job restructuring: roles in AI development, deployment, and industry applications are increasing, and short-term demand may offset some of the displacement.

The real question isn’t whether every job will disappear, but whether workers can update their skills quickly enough—and whether new jobs will emerge in industries and regions they can realistically move into.

## 7｜Two Easily Overlooked Signals

ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, Gemini, and Copilot were once all unavailable simultaneously for more than three hours—a reminder to businesses that beyond model capabilities, multi-provider redundancy and regional isolation are equally important.

The rising popularity of the open-source project everything-claude-code shows that AI-assisted programming is moving beyond “having AI write code once” toward complete workflows built around reusable agents, commands, rules, and hooks.

Today’s core takeaway: AI is no longer just a story unfolding within the tech industry. It is reallocating chip capacity, manufacturing orders, capital, and jobs—while bringing supply chains, system reliability, and investment risks to the forefront.
