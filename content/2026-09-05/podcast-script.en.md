A: The main story worth watching today is that AI competition is expanding from “who has the strongest model” to the entire industry chain: whether there are enough chips, how expensive memory is, who controls developer access, and whether services can operate reliably.

B: And this story is now deeply intertwined with financial markets. Computing orders are driving up hardware demand, while AI company valuations continue to rise. Meanwhile, inflation, interest rates, and oil prices are affecting financing costs. That’s the thread we’ll follow today.

A: Let’s start with the most immediate supply-chain pressure. According to a September 5 report by the Financial Times, AI infrastructure is consuming enormous amounts of chip capacity, tightening memory supplies and even giving rise to the term “RAMageddon.”

B: The impact goes beyond data centers. Servers can afford to pay more for high-performance memory, so production capacity naturally shifts toward higher-margin orders. That could leave consumer electronics manufacturers facing more expensive memory and longer lead times. The cost of expanding AI computing capacity is gradually making its way into the computers and phones used by ordinary consumers.

A: Meanwhile, Kioxia has proposed using upgraded NAND to handle some of the capacity requirements traditionally served by DRAM. It plans to introduce CXL modules designed for AI workloads, reducing read latency to less than one-tenth that of conventional products.

B: This doesn’t mean NAND can completely replace DRAM. The idea is to create tiered storage: frequently accessed data stays in DRAM, while other data moves to lower-cost NAND. The approach could reportedly double capacity and improve performance by 30%. If successfully deployed, both the memory cost structure of AI servers and the CXL ecosystem stand to benefit.

A: Manufacturers are already seeing orders grow. Bloomberg reports that sales at Nvidia partner Foxconn rose 52% year over year, driven by its AI server business.

B: This shows that computing investment is no longer just an expectation priced into capital markets. It is already translating into revenue from contract manufacturing, assembly, and components. Samsung’s partnership with Arm to develop next-generation on-device AI chips points to another path: using Samsung’s 2-nanometer process to move more inference onto devices such as smartphones, reducing reliance on cloud computing.

A: Platform access has also become a major battleground. Reports today say Nvidia is planning to acquire Hugging Face for approximately $12.9 billion. Hugging Face connects more than 18 million developers and hosts over 3 million models.

B: If the deal moves forward, Nvidia would gain more than a model repository. It would also acquire a model distribution channel and valuable developer relationships. The key question is whether Hugging Face can continue supporting multiple clouds, different accelerators, and open-weight models. What the community cares about most may not be gaining more resources, but how much platform neutrality can be preserved.

A: Models themselves continue to evolve toward autonomous execution. News of OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra indicates that the new model emphasizes long-term memory, tool use, and software operation, while also aiming to reduce API costs.

B: The criteria used to evaluate models will change accordingly. It will no longer be enough to ask whether a model responds like a human. We’ll need to know whether it can actually complete tasks, whether its long-term memory is reliable, how permissions are managed, and how much each completed task costs. Once models begin operating real software, capability boundaries and safety limits become equally important.

A: Another development worth watching comes from formal mathematics. Anthropic says that Peng Tianyi’s team used Claude to generate approximately 13 million lines of Lean code in 11 days, constructing 30,300 verifiable theorems and completing an end-to-end formal proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem.

B: This does not mean they rediscovered Fermat’s Last Theorem. They converted its proof into a form that machines can verify step by step. What has truly been reduced is the human labor and time required for large-scale formalization projects. If this capability becomes reliable, it could significantly accelerate both the development of mathematical knowledge bases and the verification of high-assurance software.

A: But greater capability does not guarantee reliable service. On September 3, several overseas AI services reportedly went down simultaneously for around three hours and forty minutes, while Chinese models continued operating normally during the same period.

B: This is a reminder that businesses cannot choose models based solely on benchmark rankings. Availability, regional isolation, multi-provider disaster recovery, and the concentration risks created by shared cloud infrastructure should all be part of procurement standards. Once AI is embedded in core workflows, an outage no longer means just losing access to a chat window—it can bring the entire business to a halt.

A: Finally, let’s look at the financial environment. Anthropic is reportedly preparing to go public at a valuation of $2 trillion, suggesting that valuation benchmarks for leading model companies are still rising. But hawkish signals from Federal Reserve official Beth Hammack, along with a sell-off in U.S. Treasuries, are also increasing financing pressure on lower-credit-quality borrowers.

B: Risk appetite remains relatively strong for now, and emerging markets have shown some resilience. However, developments in Iran are prompting traders to increase bullish bets on crude oil, and higher oil prices could in turn affect inflation and interest-rate expectations. Bessent, meanwhile, believes that oil could fall to $40 after the war ends, bringing bond yields down with it. That means geopolitics remains a crucial variable in market pricing.

A: To sum up today’s report: AI is simultaneously reshaping chip supplies, software access, research collaboration, and capital markets.

B: What we need to keep watching is not just model parameters, but four key issues: hardware bottlenecks, platform neutrality, service resilience, and whether these investments can generate sustainable returns in a high-interest-rate environment.
