Don’t just watch AI concept stocks. What’s really moving markets today is the collision between accelerating AI adoption and a global repricing of interest rates.

The key takeaway: AI is evolving from a technology race into a sweeping transformation of industries, fiscal policy, and cost structures. But high interest rates and geopolitical risks will keep weighing on asset valuations.

Three developments matter most.

First, Nvidia’s investment in MediaTek shows that AI chip alliances are expanding from data centers to end devices like smartphones. South Korea is also betting directly on AI with a record budget increase. The competition has now risen to the level of national industrial policy.

Second, AI is starting to reshape profits in traditional industries. Wall Street banks are demanding lower fees from major law firms. Their reasoning is simple: if generative AI boosts efficiency, clients won’t keep paying for the same billable hours.

Third, a global bond sell-off has pushed yields to their highest levels since 2008. The Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank are both signaling a more aggressive stance on rate hikes. Add rising oil prices driven by U.S.-Iran tensions, and both stocks and bonds are under pressure—with tech stocks taking the biggest hit.

So today’s main theme is clear: the long-term AI trend is getting stronger, but in the short term, interest rates and oil prices still hold the real pricing power.
