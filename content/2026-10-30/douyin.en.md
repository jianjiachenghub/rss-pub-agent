AI is still booming. So why are stocks already falling?

Today’s key takeaway: markets are starting to ask how long it will take for all that AI spending to pay off.

U.S. tech giants are reportedly spending roughly $750 billion on AI this year, funded by businesses like advertising and retail. But those established businesses are slowing. If AI profits can’t pick up the slack, cash flow will come under pressure.

Google faces that very dilemma: AI answers may keep users engaged, but they change how ads are shown and clicked. Reports suggest its latest earnings sparked concerns about AI revenue, triggering a sell-off in its shares.

Yet demand still has support. Taiwan’s third-quarter GDP grew 14.36% year over year, fueled by the AI boom. Meanwhile, Taiwanese stocks are nearing the threshold for a technical correction.

That shows industry growth doesn’t automatically mean rising stock prices. To judge where AI stocks go next, watch more than orders. Watch whether profits can keep pace with spending.
