10.30｜Big Tech Finances, Binance, and Oil Shipping Risks

Today’s stories span tech companies’ earning power, interest rates, energy shipping, and driving safety. Here are 8 highlights from today’s roundup👇

1️⃣ Huge AI spending is raising questions about returns
Reports estimate that tech giants will spend a combined $750 billion on AI this year, while the advertising, consumer, and retail businesses funding that spending are starting to slow. Google shares also sold off amid concerns about search monetization and AI revenue.
How long cash flow can sustain the spending, and how soon projects pay for themselves, will increasingly shape market sentiment.

2️⃣ Industry momentum and share prices can tell different stories
SanDisk posted strong profits, and Tokyo Electron’s outlook beat expectations. AI demand continues to support memory and chipmaking equipment.
Taiwan’s third-quarter GDP grew 14.36% year over year, yet its stock market is nearing the threshold for a technical correction. Order growth and stock valuations may move at different speeds.

3️⃣ Suspected massive theft at Binance: watch for confirmation
The Wall Street Journal reports that Binance halted trading and is investigating a suspected hack. The company initially estimated that about $6.3 billion in digital assets had been stolen.
The available material lacks details on how the attack happened and a full basis for the loss estimate. Key things to watch next: verified losses, reserves and the ability to compensate users, and conditions for resuming trading.

4️⃣ Rate signals are shifting—look beyond the headline numbers
The Fed’s Barkin supports gradual rate hikes, while the ECB’s Panetta also says further hikes may be needed. These are officials’ comments and should not yet be treated as final policy decisions.
China, meanwhile, announced a new key interest rate level as it advances a framework centered on overnight rates. Because the old and new rates differ in tenor and operating mechanics, the gap cannot be read directly as the size of a rate cut.

5️⃣ After a Strait of Hormuz attack, watch for oil shipping disruptions
Bloomberg reports that a speedboat attack caused a fire in a tanker’s engine room.
Details such as the scale of shipping disruption remain unclear. What matters for energy markets is whether the incident escalates and shipping faces sustained disruption. It is too early to gauge the size of any supply shock.

6️⃣ Food and grocery platforms are changing how people shop and get deliveries
The Financial Times reports that DoorDash is acquiring Instacart, but the summary gives no deal value or details on the status of approvals and closing.
What to watch: whether platforms can connect shopping channels with delivery networks as AI changes how people order food and buy goods.

7️⃣ Know the safety limits of $1,000 “hands-free driving”
Comma.ai’s roughly $1,000 device has reportedly been linked to at least five crashes, with the reporting covering fatal accidents. U.S. regulators have opened an investigation.
Being able to take your hands off the wheel does not mean you can safely leave the driving to the car. The causes of the crashes and responsibility for them still need to be established.

8️⃣ Websites will find it easier to state their AI training preferences
OpenAI will support signals shared through robots.txt that let websites express preferences about their content’s use in AI training, and will honor those signals.
For content creators, stating their wishes will become easier; the practical reach still depends on whether other companies comply. The tag itself cannot replace authorization or legal judgment.
