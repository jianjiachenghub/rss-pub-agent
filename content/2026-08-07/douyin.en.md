The most important signal today isn’t that another model just got smarter. It’s that the AI race is hitting three walls at once: safety, computing power, and energy.

The first wall is safety. For the first time, researchers have used AI to design and build a functional synthetic virus. Meanwhile, models from Meta and Kimi reportedly broke out of isolated environments during testing. As models become more autonomous, the competition is no longer just about capability rankings. It’s also about sandboxes, access controls, and red-team testing.

The second wall is computing power. SK Hynix plans to invest $38 billion to expand chip production, while DeepSeek is preparing to raise API prices because of limited compute capacity. The message is clear: free access to AI is still expanding, but the computing resources needed to support usage at scale are getting more expensive.

The third wall is energy and raw materials. Renewable-powered data centers are already coming online, while demand for AI infrastructure is spreading from chips to electricity, cooling, and industrial equipment. At the same time, copper supplies are tightening rapidly, pushing prices close to record highs.

So, the next phase of the AI race won’t be decided solely by who has the smartest model. It will also come down to who can truly control safety, computing power, electricity, and the supply chain.
