# Today’s 8 Must-Read Stories

September 7 brought a flood of news, but the main themes were clear: China is accelerating the localization of AI computing power, commodities continue to rise, global capital remains willing to take risks, and the tech industry is shifting from a race for speed to a race for real-world adoption.

**1｜Gold and copper rally together**

Gold prices remain elevated, while China’s central bank continues to significantly increase its gold reserves, recording its largest single purchase since 2023. Meanwhile, tariff disruptions and mining production challenges have pushed copper prices to a record high.

One reflects a shift in reserve allocation; the other signals tight physical supply. Inflation and manufacturing costs will be key areas to watch.

**2｜Domestic chips are gaining ground in the AI inference market**

DeepSeek reportedly plans to purchase at least 160,000 Huawei Ascend 950DT chips for model inference at its new data centers, while continuing to rely primarily on Nvidia for training.

This suggests AI computing could gradually split into separate training and inference markets: Nvidia retains its grip on high-end training, while domestic chips enter through the larger, recurring demand for inference. Cost per unit and cluster stability may matter more than peak performance.

**3｜AI chips have become a geopolitical choice**

Malaysia is considering using Huawei chips in AI projects, despite the possibility of pressure from the United States. When Southeast Asian countries select computing solutions, they now consider not only performance and price, but also supply security and diplomatic relations.

**4｜High interest rates have not dampened risk appetite**

Global bond yields continue to climb, yet investors are still actively allocating capital to risk assets. Policy signals within the Federal Reserve are diverging, while both the yen and the won have strengthened, putting currency markets back under the influence of policy expectations.

Markets may look buoyant, but the combination of high interest rates and strong risk appetite is unlikely to remain stable for long.

**5｜AI is entering real-world drug development and approval**

China’s first innovative drug developed with AI assistance has been approved for market launch after a full clinical process lasting three and a half years. This milestone matters more than the number of candidate molecules AI has discovered: AI-driven drug development now has a real case that has cleared China’s regulatory approval process.

However, AI’s actual contribution still needs to be assessed separately from factors such as team capabilities and trial design.

**6｜AI agents are beginning to transform R&D productivity**

OpenAI has reportedly deployed “AI research interns,” using substantial inference budgets to boost research output. The direction is worth watching, though specific productivity claims have yet to be independently verified.

The competitiveness of future R&D teams may depend not only on headcount, but also on how much computing power they can access, as well as the quality and reproducibility of agent-generated work.

**7｜Chinese flagship smartphones continue to bet on hardware-software integration**

Huawei has launched the Kirin 9050 Pro, Mate XT2, and HarmonyOS 7, with its new tri-fold phone starting at RMB 19,999. What truly matters is not the benchmark score of a single chip, but whether the chip, operating system, and on-device AI can deliver a cohesive experience.

If large models can run reliably on phones, both cloud inference costs and the competitive dynamics of flagship devices will change.

**8｜Quality and compliance are overtaking speed**

China has begun cracking down on automakers’ excessively rapid vehicle development cycles. Meanwhile, the AI browser automation tool Camofox has surged in popularity while raising risks related to anti-scraping measures, platform bans, and regulatory compliance.

From automobiles to AI agents, industries are entering the same phase: speed still matters, but stability, safety, and compliance increasingly determine how far a product can go.

Today in one sentence: **Gold and copper are pricing in scarcity, capital is still embracing risk, and the AI industry is moving from “who has the strongest model” to “who can deploy reliably at the lowest cost.”**
