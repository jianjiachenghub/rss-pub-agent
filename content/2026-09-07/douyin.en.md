Gold, copper, and Chinese-made chips are all heating up at once. Behind them lies the same signal: the world is repricing “security and self-reliance.”

The key takeaway today is that both capital and industry are shifting away from maximizing efficiency—and toward securing control over supply chains.

First, even as gold prices surged, China’s central bank made its largest single gold purchase since 2023. Meanwhile, copper also hit a record high amid tariff disruptions and mining setbacks. Gold hedges against risk; copper reflects supply anxiety. Commodities are now trading on an “uncertainty premium.”

Second, DeepSeek is reportedly planning to purchase at least 160,000 Huawei Ascend chips to power large-scale model inference. This suggests AI computing may become increasingly segmented: Nvidia retains its grip on high-end training, while Chinese chips break into the more cost-sensitive, consistently growing inference market.

Third, Malaysia is also considering Huawei chips for AI projects. Choosing chips is no longer just about performance and price—it has become a matter of geopolitical alignment.

So, there are three things truly worth watching next: whether central banks keep buying gold, whether Chinese inference chips can be delivered reliably, and whether supply-chain risks continue to push global asset prices higher.
