Today’s most important signal isn’t who released an even bigger model. It’s that AI competition is shifting from “who has more parameters” to “who delivers more intelligence per dollar.”

OpenAI revealed that by adjusting just two inference settings, GPT-5.6 tripled its score on ARC-AGI-3. That means a model’s ceiling isn’t determined by training scale alone. How reasoning state is preserved and how workflows are designed can make an enormous difference too.

The hardware side is proving the same point. SK Hynix’s profits surged, but results still missed expectations, sending its stock down nearly 13% at one point. The market believes in HBM demand—but it’s starting to ask: can these sky-high valuations keep delivering? Meanwhile, Moonshot AI is pushing optimization all the way down to the CUDA kernel level, directly competing on throughput, latency, and memory costs.

Enterprise adoption is becoming more pragmatic too. Microsoft uses specialized small security models to handle 90% of tasks, calling stronger models only for complex requests—and cutting overall costs by roughly half.

So the real deciding factor in AI’s next phase won’t just be who has the smartest model. It’ll be who can reliably turn intelligence into productivity—at the lowest cost.
