Title: Worth Watching Today: AI Spending, Cooling Inflation, and Energy Risks

August 12 brought a flood of information. Beyond new AI products, the real stories to watch are the continued surge in computing infrastructure investment, markets once again pricing in rate-cut expectations, and geopolitical risks beginning to affect energy and asset prices.

01｜AI Content Is Getting an “Invisible Signature”

Anthropic plans to add invisible watermarks to text generated by the new version of Claude, which may remain detectable even after copying or minor edits.

This could give publishers, educators, and content platforms a new traceability tool, but watermarks are not “originality detectors.” Rewriting, translation, and mixing in human-written text may all undermine detection, and a watermark cannot directly prove who the author is.

02｜AI Is Booming—and Making and Burning Money at the Same Time

Foxconn’s profit beat expectations, while Nebius’s AI cloud sales surged 514% year over year, showing that demand for computing power is spreading from chips to server manufacturing, data centers, and specialized cloud services.

On the other hand, Tencent plans to more than double its AI spending, with capital expenditure up 176% year over year, putting pressure on near-term profit growth. AI competition has moved beyond “launching models” into the stage of investing real money in computing infrastructure, user access points, and products.

An even bigger shift is that Nvidia is teaming up with BlackRock, Blackstone, Goldman Sachs, and other institutions to package “AI factories” as infrastructure assets suitable for long-term investment. In the future, computing power may increasingly resemble power plants, warehouses, and highways.

03｜Agents Are Working Around the Clock—and Starting to Make Payments

An early version of Grok Bot equips agents with cloud-based Linux virtual machines, browsers, file systems, and terminals, enabling them to execute tasks continuously.

Meanwhile, Solv Labs has demonstrated an agent payment workflow that is authorizable, verifiable, and auditable. Agents are evolving from “answering questions” to “operating computers and completing transactions.”

What will truly determine whether businesses are willing to adopt them is not just capability, but also account permissions, payment confirmation, audit trails, accountability, and human takeover mechanisms.

04｜WeChat Is Competing to Become the Super App of the AI Era

Tencent says it wants to turn WeChat into an “AI-first” ecosystem, and “Xiaowei” has already entered limited testing.

If agents can coordinate chats, payments, and various services within WeChat, the path from request to completion could become much shorter. But key questions remain: who can access which functions, how to prevent accidental actions, and how to monetize the system.

China’s agent startup scene is also heating up. Former Qwen lead Lin Junyang has founded Pragmatik Labs, with investment from HongShan, Gaorong Capital, Tencent, and others. Investors are shifting their focus from foundation models themselves to the ability to execute long-running tasks.

05｜U.S. Inflation Cools, Lifting Both Stocks and Bonds

Milder-than-expected U.S. core inflation data led markets to scale back bets on further rate hikes, sending both stocks and bonds higher.

However, one softer inflation reading is not enough to confirm a policy pivot. Upcoming employment, consumer spending, and inflation data will continue to shape the Federal Reserve’s decisions. For investors, the current environment looks more like “easing rate-hike pressure” than a confirmed shift into an easing cycle.

06｜Risks Around the Strait of Hormuz Are Rising Again

The United States and Iran are taking increasingly hard-line positions, bringing shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz back into focus.

The strait is a vital artery for global energy transportation. If tensions escalate, the impact could quickly spread to oil prices, shipping costs, inflation expectations, and risk assets. It deserves continued attention.

07｜A 14MB Small Model Brings AI to Tiny Devices

The open-source project Needle has released a foundation model of just 14MB, designed for smartphones, wearables, smart-home devices, and robots.

The benefits of small models are straightforward: fewer cloud calls, lower latency, and less private data leaving the device. But whether they can achieve real-world adoption will still depend on accuracy, power consumption, and hardware compatibility.

Today’s key trends can be summed up in three sentences:

- AI competition is entering a capital-intensive phase focused on real-world execution;
- Cooling inflation is improving market sentiment, but a policy pivot has yet to be confirmed;
- Geopolitical conflict and cross-border rules are once again reshaping energy, financing, and technology supply chains.
