Don’t just watch the latest models. AI competition has entered the stage where real money delivers real results.

Today’s key takeaway: AI is shifting from a technology narrative into a capital race built around compute, distribution, and execution.

First, demand for compute remains strong. Foxconn’s profit beat expectations, while specialized AI cloud provider Nebius saw sales surge 514%. Nvidia is even joining forces with BlackRock, Blackstone, and others to turn “AI factories” into an investable asset class. Compute is evolving from a cost for tech companies into infrastructure for long-term capital.

Second, tech giants are starting to feel the pressure of heavy investment. Tencent’s second-quarter profit growth slowed, even as capital expenditure jumped 176% year over year. Its real bet is on turning WeChat into an AI-first ecosystem—where agents could eventually orchestrate chats, payments, and services directly.

Third, agents are closing the commercial loop. Grok Bot is exploring agents that execute tasks around the clock in the cloud, while Solv Labs is adding authorization, trusted verification, and audit trails to agent payments.

So the next phase won’t be won by whoever builds the best chatbot. It’ll be won by whoever can safely and reliably get things done for users—at the lowest cost.
