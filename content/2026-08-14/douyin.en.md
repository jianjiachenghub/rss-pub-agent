Don’t just focus on whose model is stronger. The real turning point in AI is already here: competition is shifting from “more parameters and lower prices” to “owning the gateway and proving profitability.”

The clearest signal: SpaceX has reportedly acquired AI coding company Cursor for $60 billion. An aerospace giant fighting for the coding gateway shows that AI agents are becoming the new infrastructure.

Second, OpenAI’s annualized revenue has surpassed $40 billion, yet the price war keeps escalating. Meanwhile, DeepSeek has announced a threefold price increase for its flagship model. The era of buying market share with low prices may be nearing its limit. The next battle won’t be about who charges less per million tokens—it’ll be about the total cost of completing a task.

Third, Apple plans to spend nine figures licensing content from publishers to upgrade its AI-powered Siri. That means beyond models, real-time information and copyrighted content are becoming critical assets too.

So here’s today’s key takeaway: the second half of the AI race has moved beyond technology into a commercial war over access, revenue, and content. The real winners may not be the models ranked number one, but the companies that control how users enter—and turn every interaction into a sustainable business.
