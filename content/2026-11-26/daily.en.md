# Personal Daily Brief | November 26, 2026

## Today's Take

> Today's take: financing costs for AI expansion are being repriced. Whether investment translates into cash flow matters more than the scale of expansion. Rising credit-risk indicators and expectations of tighter monetary policy point to pressure on risk appetite. As platforms push their own models into workflows, competition is extending to product entry points and development productivity. Proposed easing of bank capital rules and tariff changes also leave the chip supply chain facing both funding and policy uncertainty.

## AI

### Tencent reportedly cuts external model quotas and makes Hunyuan the default

**Event:** Tencent will reportedly cut external model quotas for nontechnical roles, including product operations, to 10% starting December 1. From January 1, 2027, Hunyuan HY4 will become the default for all employees, with external models available by application.

**Why it matters:** Competition for model access is shifting from employee choice to corporate quota allocation. Changing the default will reshape spending on external models and create more internal use cases for Tencent's own offering. Whether HY4 can handle complex tasks still depends on its performance in real workflows; internal adoption alone does not establish its competitive standing.

## Investment & Finance

### SoftBank's credit risk premium tops 500 basis points for the first time in four years

**Event:** Reports on November 26 said SoftBank Group's credit risk premium exceeded 500 basis points for the first time in roughly four years. The company continues to pursue large AI investments, putting further upward pressure on its financing costs.

**Why it matters:** The cost of debt funding AI expansion is being repriced. If risk premiums keep rising, SoftBank will pay more interest to finance the same investment, raising the required return on projects and shifting market attention further toward their ability to generate cash.

### JPMorgan expects a surge in AI debt issuance next year

**Event:** JPMorgan analysts expect companies to raise $450 billion in public investment-grade bond markets in 2027 to fund AI-related capital expenditure, after which industry growth could plateau.

**Why it matters:** This forecast points to the supply of bond-market funding as a constraint on expansion. If issuance reaches that scale and business growth subsequently slows, the gap between additional interest costs and cash-flow growth will widen. Projects' ability to service debt will draw more scrutiny than capital spending alone.

### Carry-trade unwind sends peso into its longest losing streak in five years

**Event:** The Mexican peso recorded its longest losing streak in five years on Thursday as investors moved into safe-haven assets and unwound previously popular carry trades, putting selling pressure on the currency.

**Why it matters:** Currency volatility is eroding the appeal of high interest-rate differentials. Unwinding carry positions can turn shifts in risk appetite into concentrated selling. Assessing pressure on the peso therefore requires looking at rate differentials, funding costs and crowded positioning alongside domestic economic data.
