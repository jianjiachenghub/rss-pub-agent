**Nov. 26 Quick Read｜AI, Rates and Tariffs**

Six stories worth watching today—get the key points in two minutes👇

🧑‍💻 **① Tencent: Quotas are starting to shape employees’ AI choices**

Tencent will reportedly cut external model quotas for nontechnical roles, including product operations, to 10% starting December 1. From January 1, 2027, Hunyuan HY4 will become the default for all employees, with access to external models requiring an application.

The broader shift: companies are using defaults and quotas to reshape employees’ AI habits. Whether HY4 can handle complex tasks will depend on its performance in real work.

💰 **② AI investment: Markets are taking financing costs seriously**

SoftBank’s credit risk premium has risen above 500 basis points, while credit risk indicators for CoreWeave and Nvidia have also reached new highs.

Meanwhile, JPMorgan estimates that companies could raise $450 billion through public investment-grade bond markets in 2027 to fund AI-related capital spending.

Together, these developments raise a clear question: as investment grows, can project revenue and cash flow keep pace? The $450 billion figure remains a forecast.

🏦 **③ Global central banks: Further tightening remains on the table**

The Federal Reserve reportedly remains prepared to raise rates; European Central Bank officials discussed further tightening at last month’s meeting; and a hawkish Bank of Japan board member has called for an early rate hike.

Keep the distinctions clear: policy expectations, past discussions and individual board members’ remarks all still require follow-through in formal decisions.

The Bank of England, meanwhile, plans to lower bank capital requirements. That changes regulatory constraints on banks and should not be read as a rate cut.

🌏 **④ U.S.–China trade: New tariffs add uncertainty to the truce**

According to a Wall Street Journal summary, China has warned that it could retaliate if the U.S. moves ahead with proposed new tariffs on Chinese goods, potentially affecting the existing trade truce.

For now, these are proposals and warnings. The next question is whether concrete measures take effect. Anyone involved in foreign trade or cross-border business should keep watching.

💱 **⑤ Mexican peso: High interest-rate spreads cannot offset a rush for the exits**

The peso has suffered its longest losing streak in five years as investors shift into safe-haven assets and unwind popular carry trades.

A carry trade means borrowing in a low-interest-rate currency to buy higher-yielding assets and pocketing the difference. But a falling exchange rate can wipe out that interest income, while a rush to exit adds selling pressure.

🇯🇵 **⑥ Bank of Japan: Can paper gains fund real spending?**

The market value of the Bank of Japan’s ETF holdings has topped ¥100 trillion for the first time. Sanae Takaichi wants to use gains from those holdings to fund her policy agenda.

The key point: rising asset values do not immediately give the government more cash. How much can be spent depends on how gains are realized and distributed, and the pace of asset sales.
