**11.5 Today’s Highlights｜New AI Tools, a UK Rate Hike**

Today’s roundup goes beyond new tools: keep an eye on interest rates, robotics acquisitions, and corporate cash flow👇

💰 **1｜UK raises rates, keeping borrowing costs in focus**
The Bank of England voted unanimously to raise interest rates to 4%, its first hike since 2023. Fed official Hammack also said restrictive rates are still needed to contain inflation.
Inflation remains central to lending, corporate refinancing, and asset valuations.

🤖 **2｜$5.4 billion robotics acquisition blocked**
China has blocked SoftBank’s acquisition of ABB’s robotics business.
The broader takeaway: global consolidation in robotics increasingly depends on cross-border regulation and limits on technology transfers. Agreeing on a price is only part of the process; regulatory approval matters just as much.

🎨 **3｜ChatGPT can now make GIFs**
OpenAI has launched Images 2.6, which can generate GIFs from text or photos, adjust playback speed, and edit individual frames. According to the announcement, image generation is 50% faster.
That opens up more possibilities for memes, social media visuals, and simple marketing assets. Three practical measures will show how useful it is: time to a finished result, API costs, and the number of revisions needed.

🔐 **4｜Cybersecurity AI starts connecting vulnerability discovery with patching**
GPT-6 Cyber is designed for cybersecurity tasks, with an emphasis on finding vulnerabilities and developing fixes within authorized boundaries.
For security teams, the key questions are whether patches solve the problem, how many false positives arise, and how much time human review takes.

📊 **5｜AI investment faces closer financial scrutiny**
Voters in some US communities oppose data centers, Microsoft has not moved forward with a multibillion-dollar partnership with Nscale, and Apple is cutting its budget for internal AI use.
Together, these developments suggest that customer orders, operating costs, and local support are shaping the pace of AI expansion.
Market performance is also mixed: chipmakers and some data center stocks are under pressure, while some major tech companies have reportedly gained. Assessing the “AI trade” still requires looking at individual companies and business lines.

🚗 **6｜Hertz offers a reminder: watch cash flow, too**
Hertz warned of shrinking cash reserves and tightening liquidity, sending its shares sharply lower.
To assess a company’s financial health, look beyond profit to its cash burn, upcoming debt payments, and ability to raise financing.
