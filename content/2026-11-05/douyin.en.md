No matter how powerful AI gets, the numbers still have to add up.

Today’s key takeaway: AI competition is increasingly about real returns. Corporate budgets and project rollouts are both being put to the test.

OpenAI has launched GPT-6 Cyber, a model built for cybersecurity that connects finding vulnerabilities and developing patches into one workflow. But how many work hours it saves will depend on how well the fixes work—and the cost of human review.

Big companies are doing that math too. Apple is reportedly cutting its budget for internal AI use. A more practical question is taking center stage: How much value does all that tool use actually deliver?

Beyond money, the push for more computing power is also facing local resistance. In parts of the U.S., voters are backing candidates and ballot measures that oppose data centers. That means whether projects move forward also depends on where local residents stand.

So when watching the AI industry next, look beyond model capabilities and focus on three things: Will customers pay? Can costs stay under control? Can projects get off the ground?
