# Today’s Must-Read: Where Is the Money Flowing?

August 20 brought a flood of news, but the main thread was clear: AI has entered a capital-intensive arms race, while global markets are reassessing interest rates, debt, and geopolitical risks.

## 1️⃣ The AI race is becoming a contest of capital strength

Google struck a $12 billion AI chip deal with Marvell, while Waymo was also reported to be developing its own autonomous-driving chips. Custom chips are no longer merely a technical choice—they are a strategic investment measured in tens of billions of dollars.

Alibaba’s quarterly capital expenditure approached $10 billion, even as profit fell more than 75% year over year. Ant Group’s profit growth also stalled under the weight of AI investment and expansion costs. The market is no longer asking only whether companies have AI, but how quickly those investments can generate revenue.

## 2️⃣ The gateway to AI infrastructure is up for grabs

Stripe agreed to acquire OpenRouter, potentially linking model routing, usage billing, and payment settlement into a single system. Whoever controls this gateway gets closer to developers and enterprise customers.

Hudson River Trading also signed a multibillion-dollar, long-term cloud contract with CoreWeave. AI computing power is shifting from a temporary R&D expense into core infrastructure that quantitative firms must secure for the long term.

## 3️⃣ Model competition is entering a phase of “similar performance, lower prices”

Models from Moonshot and ZAI are reportedly approaching the performance of OpenAI and Anthropic, while costing about 75% less than Claude to generate usable websites.

This suggests developers may stop relying on a single model and instead automatically choose the most cost-effective option for each task. OpenAI, meanwhile, has pledged not to retain customer data—enterprise model competition has expanded from performance to price and privacy.

## 4️⃣ Long-term U.S. Treasuries are flashing a warning

Long-term Treasury prices continued to fall, and an unexpected Treasury Department buyback produced only a brief rebound. Investors’ central concerns are U.S. debt issuance strategy, long-term supply, and fiscal credibility.

One important shift is that the market is no longer absorbing U.S. debt unconditionally—it is demanding higher yields. At the same time, expectations for rate hikes in the U.S. and U.K. have cooled, but oil prices, inflation, and geopolitical risks could still make the interest-rate path volatile.

## 5️⃣ Several unusual market signals are emerging

Bitcoin climbed back above $70,000 as liquidity-driven trading recovered. Samsung and SK Hynix are preparing record shareholder returns, showing that the AI memory boom is beginning to translate into cash.

But U.S. stocks were weighed down by rising oil prices and bond yields, with Walmart plunging. In the bond market, higher-risk AT1 debt was unexpectedly the most stable segment, turning the traditional risk hierarchy upside down.

## 6️⃣ Geopolitics, regulation, and real estate cannot be ignored

U.S. pressure on Iran is extending into “economic warfare,” prompting Sweden’s central bank to keep a rate hike this year on the table. Strict AI safety measures in Massachusetts have also triggered a public disagreement between Anthropic and OpenAI.

In China, Shanghai has again eased real estate policies following an economic slowdown, while AI-related capital continues to focus on the STAR 50 Index. U.S.-Canada trade talks also show that technology and AI regulations are becoming bargaining chips between countries.

In one sentence: Today’s biggest story is not which model has topped another benchmark, but how AI is reshaping chips, cloud computing, payments, finance, and capital expenditure—while U.S. Treasuries and oil prices are repricing assets worldwide.
