The AI race has entered its “tens-of-billions spending” phase. But what decides the winner is no longer just who has the strongest models—it’s who controls the chips, computing power, and payment gateways.

The clearest signal comes from Google: it has struck a $12 billion AI chip deal with Marvell, while Waymo is also developing custom chips for autonomous driving. Tech giants are pushing the competition all the way down to the infrastructure layer.

Meanwhile, Stripe is acquiring model aggregation platform OpenRouter, aiming to combine model routing, usage billing, and payments. Whoever controls developers’ access point could control the “tollbooth” of the AI era.

But this race comes at a staggering cost. Alibaba’s quarterly capital expenditure approached $10 billion, while its profit plunged by more than 75%. The market’s next question is whether that spending can actually translate into cloud revenue and premium pricing.

The bigger shift is that Chinese models are approaching frontier-level performance at roughly one-quarter of the cost. The conclusion is clear: AI competition is moving beyond raw performance to a battle over chips, access points, and cost per unit.
