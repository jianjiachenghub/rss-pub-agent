**11.14｜6 stories worth watching today**

Today’s picks span interest rates, tech security, and world affairs 👇

1️⃣ **Dollar strengthens as markets reassess interest rates**

The dollar posted its best weekly performance in over a month, with reports attributing the gains to growing expectations of a rate hike next month. Collins mentioned “gradual rate increases,” while the Bank of England’s chief economist also called for further hikes.

Currency exchange, overseas assets, and financing costs all warrant attention. But officials’ remarks and market expectations are not final policy decisions.

2️⃣ **Cursor security incident: credentials in logs can open the door**

Cursor disclosed that keys shared with Fireworks were compromised, leading to the exposure of some credentials. A malicious extension published on October 29 may have reached up to 5,000 users before being removed about 11 hours later.

The key concern: unredacted credentials in logs can let attackers reach internal tools and extension distribution systems. When using AI coding tools, pay attention to privacy settings, extension sources, and API key permissions.

3️⃣ **As AI spending grows, watch both returns and financing**

The Federal Reserve has included risks from high AI-related valuations in its financial stability assessment. JPMorgan expects AI infrastructure spending and refinancing needs to push U.S. investment-grade corporate bond issuance to a record high in 2027.

Watch how long it takes for that spending to generate profits, and whether companies can keep shouldering financing costs. The report alone does not signal the direction of monetary policy.

4️⃣ **New claims emerge in Ukraine talks, but a deal remains distant**

Zelenskyy said the U.S. had proposed helping Ukraine join NATO in exchange for Ukrainian forces withdrawing from Donbas.

For now, this is a proposed framework relayed by Ukraine, not an agreed deal. Next, watch for an official U.S. response, NATO members’ positions, and whether the parties involved accept it.

5️⃣ **White House discussion: how to encourage AI companies to report incidents**

At a White House meeting, officials suggested easing cybersecurity-related liability policies to encourage AI companies to report critical incidents.

The practical question is how much legal exposure companies face when they voluntarily report an incident. This remains a policy proposal; details on which liabilities would be reduced and under what conditions are still pending.

6️⃣ **Two corporate stories still need key details**

On Nvidia, the supplied Bloomberg excerpt says Jensen Huang was replaced and the company’s shares recorded their worst week in over 18 months. **The leadership change still needs verification through an official disclosure**, and the material provides no succession details.

On Trump Media Group, the Financial Times reports that the company faces $1 billion in debt pressure as the outlook for its Bitcoin bet weakens. Assessing repayment risk also requires debt maturity dates, cash balances, and repayment plans.
