A: Welcome to today’s Personal Daily Briefing. The main theme to watch is “cost repricing.” From access to frontier models and API compute to bond yields, data center financing, energy shipping routes, and trade tariffs, resources once seen as reliably available are becoming more expensive and less certain.

B: This also means the AI story isn’t over, but the criteria for judging it are changing. The market is no longer asking only what the technology can do. It’s asking: Who can secure reliable access to models and compute? What is the cost of capital? And will the investment ultimately deliver business returns?

A: Let’s start with access to models. The UK is assessing how much productivity, innovation, and competitiveness could be lost if businesses could no longer use frontier AI models. Access to models is evolving from a corporate procurement issue into a factor in national competitiveness.

B: Meanwhile, Stripe will reportedly acquire OpenRouter for more than $7 billion, even though OpenRouter was valued at around $1.3 billion in May this year. If the deal goes through, Stripe would connect not only payments and billing, but also the selection, use, and settlement of more than 400 models. Becoming the default gateway for AI applications could be a major focus of the next round of infrastructure competition.

A: Beyond access, however, compute prices are also beginning to fluctuate with supply and demand. Starting August 17, DeepSeek is introducing time-of-day pricing. During peak hours, prices for some models will rise by as much as 1,100%, while prices will be cut in half at other times.

B: This has very practical implications. For businesses, model costs are no longer a fixed price list, but a variable that can be scheduled around. Summarization, analysis, and batch generation can be moved to off-peak hours, while real-time services such as customer support will have to absorb higher costs. For the first time, compute congestion is being explicitly built into the pricing mechanism.

A: Companies are also recalculating their internal AI investments. Meituan said that when it initially encouraged all employees to try AI, daily spending briefly reached tens of millions of yuan and even disrupted actual business operations. By July, CatPaw had reached 90,000 employees, powered the creation of 30,000 agents, and begun integrating into internal workflows.

B: The shift here is crucial: more calls do not necessarily mean more value. Going forward, companies are more likely to measure how many work hours AI saves, how much it shortens processes, and whether it improves operating metrics. Baidu’s revenue declining for a fifth consecutive quarter is another reminder that there is still a gap between investing in AI and turning that investment into growth—a gap that must be bridged through products, organization, and commercialization.

A: Now let’s turn to the factor having the greatest impact on asset prices today: bond yields. Inflation concerns, increased government and corporate bond issuance, and demand for AI infrastructure financing are driving a continued global bond sell-off, pushing long-term borrowing costs to their highest levels in decades.

B: AI expansion itself is also feeling the backlash. Blackstone-backed data center operator QTS has launched a bond offering, but its financing costs are high. The market used to focus on whether there were enough chips and enough electricity. Now there’s another question: Is the money needed to build this infrastructure cheap enough? Rising yields are also weighing on tech stock valuations, forcing high-priced assets—from Asian AI stocks to Nvidia—to undergo repricing.

A: Geopolitical and trade risks are pushing costs higher as well. The standoff in the Strait of Hormuz remains unresolved, and Trump has said he is in no hurry to end the war with Iran. As a result, markets are extending their expectations for how long the conflict could last.

B: The Strait of Hormuz is a vital route for global energy shipments. If shipping is disrupted, the impact could spread from oil prices to inflation, then into bond yields and corporate financing costs. Meanwhile, US–Canada negotiations are stuck over automotive rules of origin. With the August 19 deadline approaching, tariffs of up to 50% are drawing closer, forcing North American supply chains to reassess their costs as well.

A: Finally, let’s look at a few other indicators of risk appetite. Unitree Robotics is set to list on Shanghai’s STAR Market, potentially becoming a new financing model for China’s robotics industry. Meanwhile, major Bitcoin holders have stopped selling and bought a net total of around $2.9 billion over the past 60 days.

B: This shows that the market has not shifted wholesale into risk-off mode. Fund managers’ appetite for risk remains relatively high, but pressure from interest rates is already here. The real question to watch today is not whether AI or risk assets will suddenly lose their appeal. It’s which companies can still turn growth into sustainable cash flow when models, compute, energy, and capital are all becoming more expensive at the same time.
