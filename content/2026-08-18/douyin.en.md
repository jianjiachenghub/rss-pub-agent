Don’t just focus on the latest AI model. Today’s biggest shift is this: AI competition is moving from “who has the strongest model” to “who controls access, computing power, and capital.”

The clearest example: Stripe is reportedly looking to acquire OpenRouter for more than $7 billion. It’s not just after model access—it wants to combine payments, billing, and a unified gateway to over 400 models. Whoever controls that gateway could become the default distribution platform for AI applications.

Meanwhile, computing power is becoming a peak-and-off-peak resource, much like electricity. DeepSeek has introduced time-based pricing, with some peak rates surging by as much as 1,100%. Businesses can no longer compare model prices alone. They’ll need to shift usage to off-peak hours, batch workloads, and factor API costs into operational scheduling.

But the bigger pressure is coming from capital markets. Global bonds are being sold off, pushing long-term borrowing costs to multi-decade highs. Tech stocks and Asian AI-related shares are already under pressure, while financing data centers through bond issuance is getting more expensive.

So today’s conclusion is clear: the next phase of AI won’t be won by technology alone. It will be won by whoever can secure model access, optimize computing power, and obtain low-cost capital—all at once.
