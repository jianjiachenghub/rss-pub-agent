# Today’s Must-Reads: AI Price Hikes, a Bond Sell-Off, and Looming Tariffs

August 18 brought a flood of important developments. What will truly shape markets and the tech industry in the months ahead isn’t simply “which new AI was launched,” but the rising cost of compute, capital, energy, and trade.

🤖 AI: From Maximizing Usage to Measuring Real Returns

DeepSeek has introduced time-based pricing, with some API prices rising by as much as 1,100% during peak hours. This means model expenses are no longer fixed costs. Companies may start scheduling batch workloads during cheaper hours, much as they already optimize cloud-server usage.

Meituan has also reviewed the cost of getting its entire workforce to use AI. In the early days, it reportedly spent tens of millions of yuan per day while disrupting real business operations. CatPaw now serves 90,000 employees and supports 30,000 agents, with the focus shifting toward redesigning workflows. What companies really need to measure is no longer the number of calls, but how many work hours were saved and how many steps were eliminated.

Stripe is reportedly set to acquire OpenRouter for more than $7 billion. If completed, payments, model routing, and billing will converge further. Whoever controls the unified gateway to multiple models could become the “default tollbooth” for AI applications.

The UK has even begun assessing how much productivity and competitiveness it would lose without access to frontier models. Model access is evolving from a corporate procurement issue into a factor in national competition.

📉 Markets: The Bond Sell-Off Is Repricing Every Asset

Long-term bond yields worldwide have climbed to multi-decade highs, increasing borrowing costs for governments, companies, and households. Inflation concerns, demand for AI infrastructure financing, and expanding bond supply are collectively pushing interest rates higher.

High-valuation tech stocks are the first to feel the impact: the US technology sector has fallen, while Asia’s AI rally could also cool as capital becomes more expensive. Even data-center operator QTS faces higher financing costs when issuing bonds.

In short: the AI growth story remains intact, but the compute and capital supporting it are becoming more expensive.

🌍 Geopolitics and Trade: Energy Routes and 50% Tariffs Loom

Tensions in the Strait of Hormuz continue. Trump is maintaining a hard line on Iran and has said he is in no hurry to end the war. This signal will continue to affect oil shipments, freight costs, and inflation expectations.

Meanwhile, US-Canada negotiations remain stuck over automotive rules of origin. With the August 19 deadline approaching, the two sides are moving toward tariffs of up to 50%. North America’s automotive and cross-border supply chains need to prepare for a sudden surge in costs.

🚀 Industry: Robotics Gets an IPO Bellwether

Unitree Robotics is preparing to list on Shanghai’s STAR Market and could become a new financing benchmark for China’s robotics industry. If the market responds positively, the sector may see a fresh wave of listings and fundraising.

💰 Capital Flows: Risk Appetite Hasn’t Fully Receded

Despite the sharp bond sell-off, some fund managers are maintaining a relatively high appetite for risk. Large Bitcoin holders have also stopped selling, making net purchases of roughly $2.9 billion over 60 days.

But debate over the valuations of Nvidia and other AI leaders is intensifying. Bank of America believes Nvidia’s stock could trade at a discount of up to 50% due to multiple AI-related risks.

Today’s main themes are clear:

AI is entering a phase focused on calculating costs and returns; the global price of capital is rising rapidly; and energy and trade risks are adding even more uncertainty.

The most important thing to watch next isn’t which company announces the grandest AI vision, but which one can actually deliver growth amid more expensive compute, costlier financing, and an increasingly complex trade environment.
