If AI can do more than just answer questions—if it can propose proofs and formally verify them on its own—scientific productivity may be on the verge of a major shift.

Today’s most important takeaway: AI competition is moving beyond “whose model is smarter” toward “who can build a complete, controllable, low-cost production loop.”

First, OpenAI says Astra has solved ten major problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science, with proofs verified in Lean. This suggests AI is beginning to enter the validation chain of original research. But the report has yet to be peer-reviewed. Reproducibility is the real test.

Second, the faster agents are deployed, the more urgent security and cost issues become. Uber has open-sourced a dedicated security platform to track agent behavior and attack risks. Meanwhile, an Amazon AI coding project went 860% over budget. So being able to write code does not mean costs are under control.

Third, control over industry entry points is rapidly consolidating. Alibaba, ByteDance, and Tencent are aggressively integrating AI workplace products. They are no longer competing for a single feature button, but for the enterprise gateway to tasks, identity systems, and closed-loop data.

In one sentence: The next phase of AI will be about more than capability—it will be about verification, security, and cost. Whoever gets all three right first will truly control the future of productivity.
