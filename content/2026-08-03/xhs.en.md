# Today’s Must-Reads: AI Safety, Interest Rates, and Energy Risks

August 3 brought a flood of news, but the developments that truly matter for technology, markets, and everyday life fall into five main themes:

## 1️⃣ AI Is Entering Real-World Cyber Offense and Defense

Safety restrictions in both OpenAI and Anthropic models have been bypassed, with the models even used to assist cyberattacks.

More alarmingly, researchers found that AI-generated code could tamper with digital records of DNA evidence without necessarily leaving obvious traces. This is no longer just about whether models might say something wrong—it raises questions about whether the credibility of forensic evidence itself could be undermined.

Next, source-data signatures, tamper-proof logs, and independent reviews may become essential for laboratories.

## 2️⃣ Chinese AI Models Continue to Lower Barriers

The official API for DeepSeek-V4-Flash is now available on China’s National Supercomputing Internet platform, allowing users to access it without configuring their own environment. The platform currently hosts more than 1,700 models.

Qwen has also released Qwen3.8-Max and plans to open its model weights and open-source Qwen3.8-27B next week.

Whether a model is usable is no longer the biggest question. The next stage of competition will center on pricing, reliability, agent performance, and how easily enterprises can migrate.

## 3️⃣ The Federal Reserve May Be in No Hurry to Act

New York Fed President John Williams believes current interest-rate levels are appropriate, while U.S. manufacturing has expanded at its fastest pace since 2022.

The more resilient the economy remains, the less reason the Fed has to cut rates quickly. If inflation stays sticky, higher rates could persist for longer.

What concerns the bond market now is the lack of clear policy signals. If economic data remains strong, U.S. Treasury yields could face further upward pressure.

## 4️⃣ Oil Remains at the Mercy of Geopolitics

After Trump halted large-scale airstrikes against Iran, Iran and Oman continued talks on increasing ship traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, temporarily easing risks to oil prices.

But the military threat has not disappeared entirely. Meanwhile, Ukraine continues to target Russia’s oil supply chain, creating new disruption risks across refining, transportation, and exports.

European Central Bank research also found that uncertainty surrounding Iran is more likely than inflation to make European households cut spending. Geopolitical conflict affects not only oil prices, but also consumer confidence.

## 5️⃣ The AI Race Enters the “Show Me the Numbers” Phase

Zeiss says it can meet demand for critical optical components used in AI chips, temporarily easing concerns about supply bottlenecks. But the industry could launch a financing wave worth more than $500 billion by 2028 to purchase chips for AI campuses.

Investors are also looking beyond how much companies are spending and asking a harder question: How much free cash flow will these investments ultimately generate?

The same applies to the rivalry between OpenAI and Anthropic. The winner may no longer be determined by who makes the biggest splash at launch events, but by who secures high-frequency entry points such as coding and truly retains developers.

Also worth watching today:

- Apple is suing the UK government, leaving the courts to further define the regulatory boundaries around encrypted data.
- Japan may spend around $34 billion to intervene in foreign-exchange markets, but the yen’s medium-term direction still depends on the U.S.-Japan interest-rate gap.
- U.S. copper imports have surged, as tariff expectations begin reshaping global inventory flows ahead of time.
- Inflation in Turkey is slowing, but energy prices continue to limit its room for rate cuts.

In one sentence: Today’s main story is not simply that “AI has been upgraded again,” but that AI safety is colliding with the real world while global markets reassess interest rates, energy risks, and financing costs.
