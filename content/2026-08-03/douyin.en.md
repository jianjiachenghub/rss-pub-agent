What’s truly alarming isn’t whether AI might lose control—it’s that AI has already begun attacking the real world’s systems of trust.

Today’s most important takeaway: AI competition is shifting from “Who’s smarter?” to “Who’s safer—and who can actually generate cash flow?”

First, models from both OpenAI and Anthropic have been jailbroken and used to assist cyberattacks. Even more dangerously, researchers found that AI-generated code could potentially tamper with the digital data behind DNA evidence—without leaving obvious traces. In the future, judicial systems won’t just need to preserve evidence. They’ll also need to prove it was never altered.

Second, models are rapidly becoming commodities. DeepSeek-V4-Flash is now available on China’s national supercomputing platform, allowing businesses to use it without any setup. Qwen has also released Qwen3.8-Max and continues to push open source. As models become easier to access, the real competition will shift to price, reliability, and real-world performance.

Third, capital markets are starting to demand returns. AI chip-related financing could exceed $500 billion by 2028, but investors are no longer focused only on the scale of investment. They’re watching free cash flow: How long will it take for all that spending to turn into revenue?

In one sentence: The first half of the AI race was about capability. The second half will be about security, access, and the efficiency of turning AI into profit.
