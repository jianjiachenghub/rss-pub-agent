Today’s key takeaway: AI is moving beyond the “cash-burning race” and entering a phase where profits and costs are being redistributed.

On one side, tech giants have made roughly $160 billion from their stakes in AI companies. On the other, Huawei’s first-half net profit fell 37% as it ramped up AI R&D spending. Meanwhile, for every $100 model companies earn, about $35 to $40 still goes to the three major cloud providers. In other words, the more AI thrives, the most reliable profits may still flow to capital, chips, and cloud infrastructure.

The industry chain is also expanding. Nvidia plans to invest $3.5 billion in MediaTek while pushing into the global robotics market. SK Hynix, meanwhile, is considering building a plant in Japan. The race for computing power is spreading from the cloud to devices, storage, and robotics.

But the market environment remains unfriendly: rising oil prices, Treasury yields at a 19-month high, and renewed expectations of rate hikes are all weighing on stock valuations and corporate financing.

So when evaluating AI companies going forward, don’t just look at revenue growth. Watch three metrics: inference costs, capital expenditure, and who truly holds pricing power across the industry chain.
