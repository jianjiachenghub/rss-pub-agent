# Today’s Must-Reads: AI Spending, a Robotics IPO, and Shifting Global Markets

August 17 brought a flood of information. Today’s story is about more than just AI. What truly deserves attention is the accelerating investment in computing infrastructure, diverging global interest-rate expectations, and new valuation milestones for the robotics and pharmaceutical industries.

## 1️⃣ AI enters a competition of “capital + power + credit”

Nvidia plans to invest or guarantee around $100 billion in Ohio data centers serving OpenAI, and may invest up to $3 billion in energy company SB Energy.

The key question is no longer simply “who has more GPUs,” but who can simultaneously secure electricity, data centers, long-term leases, and financing. Nvidia is using its capital and credit to lock in future chip orders ahead of time.

But the risks are also emerging:

- Big Tech’s actual AI-related financial obligations may be $3 trillion higher than disclosed
- A $14 billion data center project backed by Meta and Blackstone reportedly has gaps in insurance coverage
- Alibaba, Baidu, and Kuaishou are also facing rising R&D and computing costs

The AI race is shifting from a technology contest to a cash-flow stress test.

## 2️⃣ OpenRouter’s acquisition shows the growing value of the model gateway

Stripe will reportedly acquire OpenRouter for more than $7 billion. OpenRouter aggregates over 400 models and serves more than 8 million developers.

This deal is not about competing for a particular model. It is about controlling the “routing + settlement” gateway developers use to access models. As AI agents begin purchasing services autonomously, payments may no longer happen at web checkout pages, but through machine identities, authorization systems, and automated settlement protocols.

Stripe is also using coding agents internally to generate thousands of pull requests every week. Its strategy is not simply to cut jobs with AI, but to enable the same teams to build more products.

## 3️⃣ Unitree goes public, putting robotics to the financial test

Unitree Robotics will list on the STAR Market on August 19.

This will give the humanoid robotics industry a public-market valuation benchmark. Investors will no longer focus only on whether robots can run or do backflips, but also on:

- Whether they can be mass-produced reliably
- Whether gross margins are healthy
- Whether orders are sustainable
- Whether heavy R&D investment can translate into revenue

The robotics industry is moving from the “demo moment” to the “earnings-report moment.”

## 4️⃣ Global markets: Japanese rates rise as pressure on the dollar eases

Slower economic growth in Japan is making it harder for the Bank of Japan to communicate further rate hikes. Yet markets are still betting on higher rates, with Japan’s 10-year government bond yield reaching its highest level since 1996.

Meanwhile, Goldman Sachs believes markets are pricing in an overly hawkish Federal Reserve. As expectations of renewed rate hikes cool, an index of emerging-market currencies has climbed to an all-time high.

At the same time, copper prices are nearing record highs as spot supply continues to tighten. Bitcoin is struggling to gain momentum, while Bitcoin ETFs have recorded their largest outflows in six weeks. Risk appetite is becoming increasingly selective—not every asset will rise together.

## 5️⃣ Pharmaceuticals and geopolitical risks also demand attention

WuXi AppTec’s shares have risen by around 100% due to its position as a key supplier in the GLP-1 value chain. The market is beginning to reassess whether policy risks might actually reinforce the scarcity value of a company that controls critical, hard-to-replace production capacity.

On the geopolitical front, tensions between the US and Iran continue to rise. Trump has even extended military threats to Oman, which has been acting as a mediator, while intelligence reports suggest that Iranian hardliners are adjusting their pressure tactics. Risks in the Middle East could still spill over into energy, shipping, and global markets.

Today in one sentence:

AI is evolving from a “model capability race” into a battle over capital, electricity, and financial infrastructure. Meanwhile, robotics, pharmaceuticals, interest rates, copper, and geopolitics are all sending new pricing signals.
