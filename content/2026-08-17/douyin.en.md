Today’s most important takeaway: AI competition has shifted from “who has the best model” to “who controls compute, capital, and the gateway to transactions.”

Nvidia is the clearest example. It’s not just selling GPUs—it also plans to provide investment and roughly $100 billion in credit support for OpenAI’s 10-gigawatt data center in Ohio. Capital, credit, power, and chip orders are being bundled together to lock in future demand early.

Meanwhile, Stripe is reportedly set to acquire OpenRouter for more than $7 billion. OpenRouter connects over 400 models with more than 8 million developers. Stripe isn’t chasing an ordinary platform—it’s competing for the routing and payment gateway behind AI usage.

But the risks are piling up fast. Big Tech’s true AI spending may be $3 trillion higher than reported. And Meta and Blackstone’s $14 billion data center project has also exposed major insurance gaps.

So when judging the next AI winners, don’t just look at model rankings. Watch three things: who can secure the power, who can raise the money, and who controls the final transaction gateway.
