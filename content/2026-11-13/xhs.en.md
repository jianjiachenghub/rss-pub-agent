**AI Starts Buying, Markets Want Results**

On November 13, three shifts stood out: AI began carrying out tasks, stock and bond markets repriced, and the energy supply-demand balance started to turn👇

🛒 **1｜AI Shops for You, Payment Authorization Is Key**

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, allowing agents to make purchases. Transactions require explicit user approval and retain standard bank card protections.

This means AI products need to deliver a good experience all the way through checkout: how smoothly payments go, how failures are handled, and who resolves disputes will all influence whether people keep using them.

🎙️ **2｜Search, Files, and Voice Are Easier to Build into Apps**

DeepSeek opened access to its application API, letting developers combine reasoning-powered search, file inputs, OCR, speech recognition, and speech synthesis.

Tencent Hunyuan launched Hy Realtime, which supports tool use and web search. It is already used for Yuanbao voice calls and Tencent customer service, with a cloud API planned.

For developers, these capabilities are getting easier to integrate. The next test is whether they can combine them into useful workflows with acceptable latency and costs.

🏗️ **3｜AI Infrastructure: Watch Demand and the Finances**

Cisco raised its sales forecast, and Cerebras issued quarterly sales guidance above expectations, signaling demand for networking equipment and alternative AI chips.

Meanwhile, data center developer Nscale disclosed **$5.5 billion in debt** ahead of its IPO. It still needs more cash and is increasingly reliant on Nvidia.

Assessing this business means watching whether orders turn into revenue, revenue translates into profit, and cash flow can support expansion.

📉 **4｜Money Pours In, Stocks and Bonds Remain Under Pressure**

Bank of America says global equity funds have attracted record inflows this year, with few bearish bets. Meanwhile, U.S. stocks closed lower on November 13, and China’s AI sector also came under pressure over fears of tighter chip restrictions.

U.S. Treasury yields jumped this week, adding pressure on stock valuations and corporate borrowing costs.

Watch whether money is crowding into a handful of sectors, and whether earnings can keep pace with expectations.

🌏 **5｜Chips and Regulation Are Shaping Where Investment Goes**

AI projects in Southeast Asia face the risk of further U.S. restrictions on high-performance chip exports and use. Decisions on where to build data centers increasingly need to account for delivery, licensing, and end-use requirements.

Some in the U.S. are also pushing for federal versions of state-level AI safety laws. With no specific legislative text yet, the framework for compliance across states remains uncertain.

💱 **6｜Mexico Cuts Rates, Currency Motives Remain Unclear**

Following the rate cut, traders speculated that authorities want a weaker peso to ease pressure on exports and tourism.

**For now, this is market speculation.** Central bank statements and inflation data will be needed to assess how much the exchange rate matters in policy decisions.

🛢️ **7｜Oil Markets Could Shift into Oversupply**

The International Energy Agency expects global oil markets to see their first supply surplus since the Iran war began, as Gulf oil exports recover.

If that happens, upward pressure on inflation from energy could ease. The conflict is still ongoing, though, so the pace of export recovery, shipping disruptions, and inventory changes are all worth tracking.
