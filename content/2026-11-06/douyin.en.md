AI investors are taking a harder look at the numbers.

Today’s key takeaway: markets are pricing policy and financing risks back into AI valuations.

According to the Financial Times, AI stocks sold off after the U.S. midterm elections, with $3.2 trillion wiped off the total value of Wall Street stocks. That figure reflects losses across the entire market, showing the scale of this turbulence.

More importantly, pressure is spreading to both ends of the supply chain. With Democrats now controlling Congress, Nvidia’s AI chip exports face fresh scrutiny, though details of any proposed restrictions have yet to be disclosed. Meanwhile, the European Central Bank is considering reviewing bank loans to data centers, but has yet to formally move ahead.

One is about who can buy the chips. The other is about how expansion gets funded.

That means assessing AI companies’ value now requires watching three things at once: how policy changes, whether financing can keep flowing, and whether profits can keep pace with investment.
