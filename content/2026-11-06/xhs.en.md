**Nov. 6 Daily Brief | Tech Stocks Swing, Rate Expectations Diverge**

Beyond the latest moves by tech companies, today’s focus includes capital flows, interest rates and corporate earnings. Here are five key takeaways👇

🌍 **Global Markets: Investors Are Shifting Priorities**
The Financial Times reports that U.S. midterm election results triggered a market pullback, wiping $3.2 trillion off the value of Wall Street stocks. The chip stock sell-off also spread to Asia.
The Wall Street Journal notes that some money is moving into Chinese consumer and internet stocks. India’s stock market, with its lower exposure to AI, is also drawing interest. The next question is how long this rotation can last.

🤖 **Tech Industry: Where Is Tesla Putting Its Resources?**
According to the Financial Times, Tesla will stop producing the Cybertruck as Elon Musk shifts his focus to robotics and AI. No specific date has been disclosed.
The key here is resource allocation: robotics and AI are becoming higher priorities within Tesla’s business.

🏦 **Macroeconomics: Rate Expectations Are Diverging**
Strong U.S. employment and rising wages in Japan support the case for December rate hikes, but no decisions have been made.
In Canada, unemployment rose to 6.7%, prompting traders to scale back bets on rate hikes.
To track interest rates, start with two things: whether employment is holding steady and how quickly wages are rising.

📋 **Policy and Regulation: Chip Exports and Data Center Financing Draw Scrutiny**
Nvidia’s AI chip exports are facing fresh scrutiny from the U.S. Congress, though no specific restrictions have been disclosed. The European Central Bank is considering a review of banks’ data center lending, but this remains under consideration.
In China, the State Council called for stronger efforts to stabilize the stock market. Specific policy tools will be worth watching.

💼 **Business: Earnings Expectations Move Share Prices**
DraftKings shares fell 29% after the company lowered its earnings outlook, prompting investors to reassess its ability to generate profits.
Meanwhile, Brookfield, which is expanding into AI, raised $25 billion and reported higher profits. But the summary did not fully break down how the funds would be used, so the entire amount cannot be counted as AI investment.
