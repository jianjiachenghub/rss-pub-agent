Oct. 27 Daily Brief｜Cheaper AI, Pressure on Automakers and Workers

Today’s key themes: AI at work, corporate cost cuts, and the risks behind big money👇

1️⃣ AI gets cheaper—and moves closer to everyday office work
OpenAI has released GPT-6.1 Terra, cutting standard API prices by 50% from the previous generation. It is also expanding its partnership with Oracle, with plans to bring business applications into ChatGPT.
Using business tools through chat could become easier. But whether it’s worthwhile will depend on the cost per completed task and how much human intervention is needed.

2️⃣ Mercedes cuts its profit outlook
Weak demand in China and rising costs have prompted Mercedes to lower its full-year profit forecast. Its passenger car division now expects an adjusted return on sales of 2%–3%; the company previously reported a 31% decline in third-quarter sales in China.
Even luxury brands face pressure from both demand and costs.

3️⃣ Microsoft offers voluntary exits to 60,000 employees
Corporate cost cuts are reshaping staffing. The 60,000 figure refers to employees eligible for the offer; the actual number of jobs cut will depend on how many accept. Skydance is also pursuing similar changes.

4️⃣ Private equity-owned companies face a major debt test
About $300 billion in debt is set to come due in a wave starting in 2028, and fund managers are seeking loan extensions.
What to watch next: whether companies can refinance smoothly, and how the terms affect profits.

5️⃣ Read Anthropic’s huge IPO reports alongside its cash flow
Anthropic reportedly plans to raise up to $100 billion at a target valuation of $2 trillion.
Its $80.7 billion first-half net loss came mainly from non-cash adjustments. Assessing its funding needs also requires looking at payment obligations under more than $650 billion in multiyear contracts.

6️⃣ A new ruling in Alibaba’s legal battle
A U.S. judge has blocked a ban that prohibited the Pentagon from doing business with firms lobbying for Alibaba.
The ruling concerns a specific business restriction. Alibaba’s challenge to its inclusion on a military-linked blacklist remains one to watch.

7️⃣ Borrowing against stocks is now a $5 trillion market
Deloitte research says loans secured by shares are becoming available to less wealthy borrowers.
Stock holdings can provide access to cash, but falling share prices may force borrowers to post more collateral or repay their loans.

8️⃣ Corporate security checks need to cover third-party office software
In a security test authorized by Anthropic, an AI agent accessed internal information, including payroll and job offers, through third-party enterprise software in under 12 hours.
Companies need to close security gaps in how permissions connect across systems.
