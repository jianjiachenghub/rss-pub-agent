AI’s cash burn is already affecting credit ratings.

Today’s key takeaway: The AI race is entering a cash-flow test. Whether revenue can keep up with spending will shape how fast the tech giants can keep expanding.

Bloomberg reports that five major companies in the data center race have committed more than $3 trillion over the coming years. Once built, those facilities will need sustained demand from paying customers to deliver returns.

The financial strain is already showing. S&P revised Meta’s credit rating outlook to negative because of its heavy AI spending, warning of a possible downgrade within two years. For now, only the outlook has changed.

Amazon, meanwhile, has arranged up to $80 billion in supplier financing to give itself more cash-flow flexibility for hardware purchases. But that limit isn’t the amount actually borrowed, and any borrowing still has to be repaid from future cash flow.

So as you watch AI companies, look beyond computing capacity. Track demand from paying customers, how quickly cash comes back in, and debt repayment pressures. How much revenue that spending generates will determine how far this race can go.
