# Nov. 8 Daily Brief｜Big Tech Financing, Elections, and Carbon Markets

Today’s stories span tech expansion, finance, international politics, and climate policy. Here are 6 developments worth watching👇

**1️⃣ As computing capacity expands, power needs to keep up**
According to the Financial Times, Anthropic has agreed to spend $3.4 billion on gas-fired generators. ByteDance is also moving ahead with plans to expand its data centers in Brazil.

The takeaway is straightforward: how quickly data centers come online depends on power, equipment, and construction timelines. Planned capacity still takes time to become available.

**2️⃣ Meta issues $25 billion in bonds—watch the borrowing costs**
According to Bloomberg, Meta has issued $25 billion in corporate bonds, bringing tech companies’ massive financing needs into the credit markets.

The amount grabs attention, but assessing the financing terms requires details on coupon rates, maturities, and investor demand. The available material does not disclose those details or establish how the funds will be used.

**3️⃣ Where will the impact of the U.S. election be felt?**
According to BBC Chinese, Democrats have regained both chambers of Congress, while $14 billion in arms sales to Taiwan still await Trump’s approval. Bloomberg is also examining challenges facing Trump-linked deals in Latin America.

Next, watch the pace of arms-sale approvals, chip restrictions, and policy oversight. The election results alone do not establish that policy has already shifted.

**4️⃣ Linking UK and EU carbon markets could affect business costs**
According to Bloomberg, an agreement to link the UK and EU emissions trading markets is in its final stages.

If implemented, it could boost market liquidity, narrow carbon-price gaps, and reduce carbon compliance costs for some cross-border trade. Actual savings will depend on the scope of mutual recognition, the start date, and the final terms.

**5️⃣ Chinese AI models seek distribution, while search takes on tasks**
China Mobile and Zhipu have signed an agreement to bring the GLM model family to China Mobile Cloud’s MaaS platform, opening a new channel to enterprise customers.

Baidu’s search upgrade, introduced on October 30, showcased tasks such as college admissions planning, trip budgeting, and hotel price-drop alerts.

Taken together, these developments show that product competition increasingly hinges on three questions: Where will customers come from, can the product complete their tasks, and can revenue cover costs?

**6️⃣ The faster AI writes code, the more review matters**
On October 15, the Claude Code team disclosed that AI writes roughly 90% of its own code, supported by six layers of review and a rapid rollback process.

From June through September, the number of merged PRs more than doubled, while the share requiring rollbacks fell by over 40% in relative terms. A lower share does not necessarily mean fewer rollbacks overall. For development teams, the useful lesson is how to scale review, testing, and rollback capabilities alongside output.
