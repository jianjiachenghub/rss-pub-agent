What truly deserves attention isn’t any single AI headline—it’s that tech giants are locking down capital, computing power, and distribution all at once.

Nvidia reportedly plans to acquire Hugging Face for $13 billion, expanding beyond chips into open-source models and the developer gateway. DeepSeek is planning large-scale purchases of Huawei AI chips, showing that domestic computing power is moving into the core of leading model expansion. ByteDance, meanwhile, has secured nearly $30 billion in low-cost syndicated loans, adding firepower for up to $70 billion in annual capital spending.

At the same time, interest rates are once again pressuring risk assets. Strong U.S. jobs data has pushed markets to raise their rate-hike expectations, sending Bitcoin below $80,000. But the next move is far from certain—the upcoming inflation data will determine the direction.

In one sentence: AI competition has evolved from “Who has the strongest model?” to “Who can control capital, chips, and the developer ecosystem for the long haul?”
