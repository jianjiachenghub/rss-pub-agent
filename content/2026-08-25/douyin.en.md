Today’s biggest signal: AI competition is shifting from “racing to secure Nvidia chips” to “rebuilding the entire supply chain.”

OpenAI says its in-house chip has outperformed Nvidia’s in testing. The model and test conditions haven’t been disclosed, but the direction is clear: leading companies don’t want to stay dependent on external computing power forever.

Meanwhile, chipmakers aren’t the only ones cashing in. Taiwan’s King Slide Works is riding the data center boom with server rack rails—making its founder Taiwan’s richest person. The AI gold rush is spreading to essential components like cooling systems, connectors, and server racks.

At the other end, Xiaomi has launched a dedicated on-device AI chip designed to tackle memory bandwidth and inference costs when running large models on smartphones. In the future, more AI tasks may be completed directly on devices—without being uploaded to the cloud.

So today’s takeaway is this: in the next phase of AI, the opportunity won’t just depend on who has the strongest model. It will also depend on who controls the chips, devices, and critical components. Value across the computing supply chain is being redistributed.
