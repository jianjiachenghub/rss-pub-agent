Host A: But equipment delivery is only the first step. Data centers also need reliable power, network connectivity, and customer demand. Meanwhile, Taiwan has charged nine people suspected of reselling AI servers to mainland China, showing that export controls have expanded beyond chips themselves to complete systems, end users, and supply-chain employees.

Host B: Turning to on-device computing, Xiaomi has unveiled its Xring O100 AI accelerator, designed primarily for large-model inference on smartphones and other devices. Its key focus is high memory bandwidth, with the goal of allowing more tasks to be processed locally instead of being uploaded to the cloud.

Host A: That could improve response times and privacy while reducing cloud costs. But beyond the specifications, we still need to see its power consumption, thermal performance, manufacturing yield, and whether it can truly become part of the smartphone and app ecosystem. The outcome of the on-device AI race won’t be determined by chips alone.

Host B: Competition for the workplace gateway is also heating up. ByteDance has folded the TRAE and Coze teams into the Doubao organization and launched “Doubao Work,” hoping to bring AI agents into enterprise collaboration and programming.

Host A: Consolidating resources makes sense. The difficult part is defining how Doubao, Feishu, TRAE, and Coze will divide responsibilities. Another, more sensitive issue is data access: some companies want AI to read employees’ Slack messages for search and summarization, but internal communications often contain personnel information, customer data, and trade secrets. The more capable AI agents become, the more important access controls and auditing will be.

Host B: Medical AI also saw progress today that is closer to real-world use. DAMO Academy and its hospital partners developed a liver cancer model called DAMO LiON. During a two-month prospective trial, it detected fifteen malignant tumors in CT scans that had previously been missed, most of them around one centimeter in size.

Host A: What matters is that this wasn’t just a matter of chasing metrics on historical data—the model was tested within a real clinical workflow. The next things to watch are multicenter trials, false-positive rates, liability, and medical-device approval. For medical AI to succeed in practice, the quality of the evidence matters more than any single accuracy figure.

The AI story is still advancing, but investors are beginning to distinguish between genuine demand, supply-chain gains, and overcrowded trades.
