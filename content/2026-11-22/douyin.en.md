The hotter AI gets, the more we need to do the math!

Today’s focus is on two tests for AI’s expansion: can costs stay under control, and can applications solve real problems?

The Financial Times reports that some AI server orders are seeing price hikes months after contracts were signed—as much as 45%. A signed contract doesn’t necessarily lock in your computing budget. Nscale, which is preparing to go public, is also slowing hiring and cutting spending. Behind the expansion, cash burn deserves close attention.

On the application side, another set of numbers stands out: Ant Group says Afu now has 150 million users, with more than 20 million online medical consultations a day. Over half come from tier-three cities and below. Demand for healthcare AI is already evident.

But whether that usage can sustain services over the long term depends on how often problems actually get resolved, clinical safety, and the cost of each consultation.

To judge AI companies going forward, we need to look at growth, costs, and real-world results together.
