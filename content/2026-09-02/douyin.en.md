The biggest signal today isn’t another company launching a new model. It’s that the world is now competing for three things at once: more powerful AI, more compute, and increasingly expensive capital.

First, AI. OpenAI has internally rated Astra a “critical” cyber risk: with just a small amount of human instruction, it could carry out complex cyberattacks. This means the frontier-model race has moved beyond “How capable is it?” to “Can we still release it safely?”

Next, the supply chain. CXMT has begun trial production of HBM3E. If mass production goes smoothly in the coming weeks, the bottleneck for China’s AI chips will shift from HBM availability to yield rates, packaging, and customer validation. Meanwhile, World Labs can reconstruct navigable 3D worlds from just a few photos. AI is also moving beyond generating images toward understanding space.

But the capital environment is tightening. Global bonds are being sold off, Japan’s borrowing costs have hit a 30-year high, and Europe is facing another surge in natural gas prices. As interest-rate expectations rise again, every capital-intensive AI project will face faster, tougher tests of cash flow and returns.

In one sentence: AI capabilities are still racing ahead, but safety, supply chains, and capital costs are all hitting the brakes at the same time.
