AI is getting cheaper, but access to computing power is tightening. Today’s key takeaway: the AI race is about both access and getting things done.

Bloomberg reports that the U.S. is taking steps to restrict China’s remote access to Nvidia AI chips. The controls now extend to remote computing power, but the terms and enforcement details haven’t been disclosed.

Meanwhile, Tencent’s Hunyuan has launched Hy4 flash, supporting a million-token context window, with a reported cost per task just one-thirteenth that of the Hy4 preview. But actual savings still depend on task success rates and speed.

And OpenAI has added a computer-use agent to its healthcare version of ChatGPT, letting AI work with existing medical software and handle real-world workflows.

Taken together, these stories raise two questions businesses need to answer next: can they reliably access computing power, and how much does it really cost for AI to complete a task?
