The biggest story today isn’t another company launching a new model. It’s that the AI industry is collectively moving beyond “cheap expansion” and entering a new phase of rising costs across capital, computing power, and commercialization.

The clearest signal comes from DeepSeek: it plans to sharply raise prices for its AI services while reviving an $8 billion funding round. Raising prices and securing funding at the same time shows that competition among Chinese foundation models is shifting from price wars to revenue and financial strength.

The second signal is the continued surge in computing investment. Alphabet plans to issue up to $25 billion in bonds, while an Emirati fund is considering investing $6.3 billion in Japanese AI data centers. The AI race increasingly resembles a capital-intensive war, where the winners will be determined not only by model performance, but also by who can sustain infrastructure costs over the long term.

The third signal comes from the memory industry. CXMT has rejected Apple’s push for lower prices, insisting its quotes remain no lower than those of Samsung and SK Hynix. As leading manufacturers shift toward higher-margin AI memory, the supply of general-purpose memory is tightening—and bargaining power is moving toward suppliers.

So today’s key takeaway is this: AI demand isn’t cooling, but the era of free services, low prices, and asset-light growth is fading. Next, rising service prices, chip and memory costs, and financing expenses could continue spreading both upstream and downstream across the AI value chain.
