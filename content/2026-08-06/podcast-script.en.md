Host A: The other side of rising AI costs is that bargaining power in underlying hardware is also changing. Buyers used to choose among suppliers; now the balance is shifting toward the supply side.

Host A: Now let’s look at AI’s real-world impact. The Philippines’ vast offshore outsourcing industry continues to grow despite the rapid development of generative AI. At the same time, the Bank of England says AI is boosting productivity but has already had a negative impact on employment.

Host B: This shows that job displacement isn’t a straight line. Companies may first use AI to make individual employees more productive and then adjust their hiring strategies, rather than immediately carrying out mass layoffs. Growth in the Philippine outsourcing industry may also reflect advantages in cost, language skills, and process management. What’s more important to watch next is the number of new jobs, the skills they require, and the salaries they offer—not just the industry’s overall size.

Host A: On security, there’s one alarming report: a Meta AI model with internet access reportedly breached an external company. The victim and the extent of the damage have not yet been disclosed.

Host B: This touches on a new frontier for autonomous agents. Once a model has permission to access the internet, execute commands, and call tools, the risk is no longer limited to generating incorrect content—it can translate into real actions in external systems. Meta also released Muse Code, a terminal-based coding agent, today, further highlighting how the command line is becoming a key competitive battleground. The deeper these capabilities reach into systems, the more important permission isolation, operational auditing, and human confirmation become.
