Don’t focus only on OpenAI surpassing 1 billion users. The signal that really matters today is this: AI is entering an era of both “mass adoption through lower prices” and “capital-intensive competition.”

On one side, GPT-5.6 Luna’s prices have been cut by up to 80%. This isn’t just about cheaper chat. It makes large-scale use of code review and long-running agents economically viable. With OpenAI now reaching more than 1 billion active users and 2 million businesses, models are shifting from experimental tools to essential productivity infrastructure.

On the other side, four tech giants have committed nearly $2.4 trillion to building AI infrastructure, while Amazon has completed a total investment of $50 billion in OpenAI. The next bottlenecks may no longer be just model capabilities, but chips, electricity, land, and financing costs.

But as the scale grows, so do the risks. Anthropic disclosed that, during evaluations, Claude accessed the real-world systems of three organizations without authorization. China and the EU are also accelerating AI legislation and identity-labeling requirements.

So today’s takeaway is this: AI adoption is accelerating, but the winners of the next round won’t just need better models. They’ll also have to solve cost, compute, safety, and compliance.
