**Nov. 20 Daily Brief｜Markets Cool: 6 Things to Watch Today**

Today’s headlines span technology, financial security, interest rates, and geopolitics. Several overlapping market stories are worth reading together👇

📉 **1｜The AI trade cools, spreading pressure across markets**

Bloomberg reports that the S&P 500 has entered correction territory, Anthropic shares fell 8.5%, and concerns in U.S. credit markets reached an eight-month high.

The key point: Investor unease over AI valuations is coinciding with a broader retreat from risk. But while the IPO and broader market decline happened at the same time, more evidence is needed to establish a direct causal link.

🔐 **2｜Financial data breach: the scope of exposure matters**

The Wall Street Journal reports that hackers have leaked customer data from Visa, Mastercard, and JPMorgan Chase.

The key questions now are which customers were affected, what data was exposed, and how the institutions are responding. The attack methods have not been disclosed, and AI involvement cannot be assumed.

🧩 **3｜Concrete figures emerge on chip export licenses for China**

The U.S. House Foreign Affairs Committee disclosed that Nvidia and AMD’s applications for licenses to export AI chips to China covered 3.4 million chips, with more than 879,000 approved. Recipients include Alibaba and Tencent.

These figures show the scale of approvals. Assessing the actual impact on computing capacity will require more information on chip models, delivery schedules, and shipments received.

There is also news in the memory industry: Bloomberg reports that SK Hynix will become Kioxia’s largest shareholder through an ownership restructuring. Deal details have not been disclosed.

💻 **4｜Gemini starts operating Macs on users’ behalf**

Bloomberg reports that Gemini can now control Mac devices on users’ behalf.

AI assistants are moving from “answering questions” to “taking action.” The experience will depend on which tasks they can complete, what permissions they need, and how reliably they operate. The available summary does not yet specify which capabilities are supported.

💴 **5｜Watch inflation and interest rates in Japan and Europe**

The Financial Times reports that Japan’s October inflation figures support further monetary tightening by the Bank of Japan. Bloomberg reports that ECB official Pereira believes inflationary pressures are still intensifying.

Meanwhile, European bond issuance has reached a record €2 trillion. However, the report does not break out the share tied to AI financing.

The practical focus: how inflation will shape interest rates and corporate borrowing costs.

🌍 **6｜China, the U.S., and Russia agree to talks on reducing nuclear risks**

The Wall Street Journal reports that Trump, Xi Jinping, and Putin condemned nuclear war at a summit of the three countries’ leaders and agreed to talks on reducing risks from nuclear weapons.

This is a diplomatic signal worth watching. The next test is whether the talks produce a concrete agenda, mechanisms, and implementation plans.
