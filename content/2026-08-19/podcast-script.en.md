Host A: Next is the financial pressure behind computing capacity. AI cloud computing company Nebius plans to issue $4.5 billion in convertible bonds to fund data center construction. The advantage is relatively low near-term interest pressure, but the trade-off is potential dilution of shareholder equity in the future.

Host B: And long-term yields are rising, making it more expensive to borrow for data centers while also raising the discount rates applied to technology company valuations. In other words, AI projects can no longer rely solely on promises of long-term demand. They need to demonstrate equipment utilization, cash flow, and payback periods more quickly.

Host A: The U.S. Treasury’s resumption of Treasury bond buybacks also reflects official concern over long-term financing costs. After the operation, Treasury yields fell, and the dollar dropped to a three-month low. Institutions including BlackRock and Aviva have shifted toward short-term bonds, deliberately avoiding volatility at the long end.

Host B: However, buybacks can improve market liquidity without necessarily changing inflation, bond supply, or the term premium. If long-term interest rates remain elevated, the pressure will affect more than government financing. AI infrastructure and richly valued technology stocks will also feel the strain.
