The real bottleneck in the AI race is no longer the model—it’s money, power, and the authority to act.

The biggest shift today: AI is moving from “answering questions” to “getting things done.” Amazon has officially launched agentic payment capabilities, allowing AI to make transactions autonomously within spending limits, audit requirements, and risk controls. In the future, AI agents won’t just recommend products—they may place orders and pay for them on your behalf.

But the more power agents gain, the higher the infrastructure bill. Nebius plans to issue $4.5 billion in convertible bonds to build data centers, while AI and chip costs are squeezing profits at Baidu and Xiaomi. Meanwhile, persistently high long-term interest rates are driving up financing costs for computing projects and pushing down tech valuations.

Even more critical is electricity. New nuclear reactor projects in the U.S. are increasingly tied to data-center demand, while tech giants must also spend money to win community support. Only those that secure reliable power, low-cost capital, and construction permits will earn a place in the next round of the AI race.

In one sentence: The second half of the AI race won’t be decided by model performance alone, but by who can let agents spend money safely—and secure capital, electricity, and social license at the lowest cost.
