Here’s today’s key takeaway: AI is breaking through three boundaries at once—the limits of models, capital, and infrastructure.

First, Meta is returning to open AI. Zuckerberg announced more open-weight models, including new models that can run directly on laptops. AI access is moving from cloud APIs to personal devices, improving costs and privacy—but making it harder for platforms to manage risks centrally.

Second, AI agents are moving away from human approval. Claude Code will enable automatic mode by default after research found that humans identify only 13.6% of dangerous commands. The future of AI safety isn’t about making people click “confirm” at every step. It’s about whether the system can truly detect high-risk behavior.

Third, the compute race has become a race for capital and energy. Anthropic is partnering with Macquarie and Singapore’s GIC to build data centers, while Microsoft is expanding production of its in-house AI chips. At the same time, sharp fluctuations in AI workloads are causing data center equipment failures.

So the winners of the next phase may not simply be the companies with the strongest models. They’ll be the players that can solve four challenges at once: open ecosystems, autonomous safety, long-term financing, and stable power.
