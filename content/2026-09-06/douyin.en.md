Today’s most important takeaway: AI competition has shifted from “whose model scores higher” to “who can put agents into real production—while keeping costs and risks under control.”

The strongest signal comes from Claude: in just 11 days, it generated roughly 13 million lines of Lean 4 code, turning Fermat’s Last Theorem into a proof that computers can verify line by line. Mathematical reasoning is becoming an engineered product—but at this scale, maintaining and reviewing the code creates a whole new challenge.

Meanwhile, GPT-6 Astra is rolling out at scale to paid users and through the API. It can operate browsers, redesign workflows, and coordinate multiple agents to complete complex tasks continuously. But as its cybersecurity capabilities grow stronger, companies are asking a bigger question: can it be monitored from start to finish?

This shift is already reaching the talent and capital markets. UBS is starting to require junior bankers to have AI expertise, while traders are hunting for cheaper AI plays—and turning their attention to Chinese stocks.

So what becomes truly scarce next may not be model capability alone, but three things: verifiability, oversight, and the ability to operate in real-world business.
